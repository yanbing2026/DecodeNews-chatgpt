# 北卡罗来纳州Chili’s顾客误饮清洁剂调制的玛格丽塔，五人出现化学灼伤

美国北卡罗来纳州 Forest City 一家 Chili’s Grill & Bar 餐厅发生清洁剂误入饮品事件，三名顾客和两名员工因接触被误当作糖使用的清洁化学品而出现口腔化学灼伤。当地卫生部门称，餐厅已在完成清洁和检查后恢复营业，调查已经结束，没有开出罚单。受伤者接受了医疗处理，但其具体情况未公开。

## 清洁剂被误当成糖使用

NBC News 11月2日报道，事件发生在10月28日。卫生部门称，一名员工在午餐营业前准备酒吧时，发现一个没有标签的容器，误以为里面是糖，将其中的颗粒状脱脂清洁剂用于玛格丽塔饮品区域。三名顾客点饮料后很快出现不适，口腔及周围部位发生化学灼伤。两名员工随后尝试该物质，也出现类似症状。

## 化学品如何进入餐厅

Foothills Health District 表示，10月26日，一名负责外部高压清洗的承包商将颗粒状脱脂剂交给餐厅员工。员工把化学品放进未贴标签的容器，之后容器被带入餐厅并留在酒吧区域。两天后，另一名员工误把容器中的物质当作糖使用。卫生部门没有披露化学品的具体名称。

## 卫生部门暂停营业后恢复许可

事件发生后，餐厅一度被要求停止营业，卫生人员要求工作人员彻底清洗、冲洗并消毒受影响的食品准备区域和设备。WBTV 报道，卫生部门随后恢复了餐厅的食品服务许可。NBC News 称，餐厅在第二天重新开放，卫生部门发言人表示调查已经结束，没有开出罚款。

## 伤者人数存在企业与官方口径差异

卫生部门统计为三名顾客和两名员工，共五人出现症状。Chili’s 发言人则在邮件声明中称，事件造成两名顾客和员工受伤，但没有解释与卫生部门公布数字之间的差异。卫生部门表示，伤者接受了医疗处理，但发言人没有掌握其后续健康状况。由于公司和政府统计口径不同，报道应保留双方说法，而不能将其中一方数字视为已解决的最终统计。

## 食品服务中的标签和储存问题

Foothills Health District 指出，事件反映出食品服务场所正确标记、储存和处理化学品的重要性。清洁剂不应被放置在可能与食品或饮料原料混淆的容器中。此次调查所披露的链条包括承包商交付化学品、员工使用无标签容器，以及另一名员工误认容器内容物。卫生部门未公布具体化学品名称或全部内部操作记录。

这起事件发生在单一 Forest City 门店。Chili’s 表示已支持受影响人员，并处理该门店的相关问题。现有报道没有说明伤者是否完全康复，也没有公布餐厅是否面临进一步民事索赔。当前已确认的是误用清洁化学品导致多人受伤，餐厅经卫生检查后恢复营业。

# Five People Suffer Chemical Burns After Cleaning Product Is Mistaken for Sugar at a North Carolina Chili’s

Three customers and two employees suffered chemical burns after a cleaning product was mistakenly used in margaritas at a Chili’s Grill & Bar in Forest City, North Carolina. The Foothills Health District said the substance was a granulated degreasing chemical that had been placed in an unlabeled container. The restaurant was temporarily closed, then allowed to reopen after inspectors reviewed the incident and required cleaning. The health district said its investigation was closed and no fines were issued.

## A cleaning chemical was mistaken for sugar

NBC News reported on November 2 that the incident occurred on October 28. According to the health district, an employee preparing the bar for lunch service found an unlabeled container and believed its contents were sugar. The employee used the chemical at the margarita station. Three customers who ordered drinks shortly after the restaurant opened experienced discomfort and chemical burns in and around their mouths.

Two restaurant employees later tasted the substance and reported similar symptoms. The health district’s account identified three affected customers and two employees. The exact chemical name was not disclosed in the reports.

## How the chemical entered the restaurant

The health district said that on the morning of October 26, a restaurant employee received a granulated degreasing chemical from a contractor who was power-washing the building’s exterior. The employee placed the substance in an unlabeled container. For reasons that were not explained in the public account, the container was brought inside and left in the bar area.

Two days later, another employee mistook the contents for sugar and used it in preparing the margarita station. The sequence described by investigators points to failures in labeling, storage and communication. The available reporting does not identify the chemical’s brand or provide a full internal account of how the container was moved.

## The restaurant was temporarily closed and then reopened

After the exposure, the restaurant was shut down while health officials addressed the contamination risk. WBTV reported that inspectors required staff to thoroughly wash, rinse and sanitize affected food-preparation areas and equipment. The health district later reinstated the restaurant’s food-service permit.

NBC News said the restaurant reopened the following day. A health district spokesperson said the investigation had been closed and that no fines were issued. The reports do not describe any additional enforcement action against the restaurant or the contractor.

## The company and health district gave different injury counts

The health district said three customers and two employees experienced symptoms. A Chili’s spokesperson described the incident as one that resulted in injuries to two guests and team members, without explaining the difference between the company’s count and the health district’s account.

The health district said the injured people received medical treatment, but its spokesperson did not have information about their conditions. The company said it took immediate action to support those affected and address the circumstances at the Forest City location. The differing counts should remain attributed to the respective sources rather than being treated as a resolved discrepancy.

## Chemical labeling and storage are central concerns

The Foothills Health District said the incident highlights the importance of proper chemical labeling, storage and handling in food-service establishments. Cleaning products should be kept in clearly marked containers and separated from ingredients used to prepare food and drinks.

The publicly described sequence involved a contractor providing the chemical, an employee placing it in an unlabeled container and another employee mistaking it for sugar. The health district did not disclose the chemical’s exact identity or every internal procedure involved.

Chili’s said the incident was isolated to its Forest City restaurant and that it had addressed the issues specific to that location. The available reports do not establish whether the injured people have fully recovered or whether any civil claims will follow. The confirmed facts are that five people were affected according to the health district, the restaurant reopened after inspection, and no fines were issued.
