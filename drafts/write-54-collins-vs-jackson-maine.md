# 缅因州参议院选战可能进入排序选择计票，Collins与Jackson竞争激烈

缅因州共和党参议员 Susan Collins 与民主党候选人 Troy Jackson 的选战可能无法在投票当晚确定结果。根据 New York Times、Portland Press Herald 与 Siena Research Institute 的最新民调，Jackson 在714名可能投票者中以51%对45%领先Collins，误差范围为4.3个百分点。由于缅因州采用排序选择投票，若没有候选人获得超过半数首选票，选举将进入后续排序计票。

## 最新民调显示Jackson领先六个百分点

Portland Press Herald 11月1日报道，最新联合民调显示，Jackson获得51%支持，Collins为45%。该调查访问了714名可能投票的缅因州选民，误差范围为4.3个百分点。与9月同一调查中Collins领先Jackson三个百分点相比，最新结果出现了九个百分点的变化。民调结果反映调查时点的选民倾向，不是正式投票结果。

## 排序选择制度可能延长计票

USA Today 报道，缅因州在有两名以上候选人的州级选举中使用 ranked-choice voting。除Jackson和Collins外，该场选举还有六名登记的写入候选人。如果没有候选人获得超过50%的首选票，计票程序将进入排序选择阶段，根据选民的后续偏好重新分配选票，直到有候选人达到胜选门槛。

## 选举结果未必在投票夜揭晓

缅因州州务卿 Shenna Bellows 对 USA Today 表示，考虑到民调接近和写入候选人数量，进入排序选择计票的可能性较高。如果需要进行后续计票，结果可能需要数天才能完成。选民在选票上如何排序候选人，将影响第二轮及之后的票数分配。

## 经济议题成为选民关注重点

Portland Press Herald 报道称，民调受访者将经济列为最重要议题。Jackson在生活成本议题上的信任度略高于Collins，而Collins在移民议题上仅领先一个百分点。调查还显示，Jackson在未登记党派选民中获得52%支持，Collins为41%。这些数据反映受访者在调查期间的倾向，并不能直接预测最终投票。

## 参议院多数席位增添全国意义

缅因州这场选举受到全国关注，因为其结果可能影响美国参议院控制权。Collins是长期任职的共和党参议员，Jackson则是民主党候选人。民调领先并不等于胜选，排序选择程序也可能改变首轮投票后的排名。最终结果仍需等待投票结束、首选票统计和必要的后续计票。

# Maine Senate Race Between Susan Collins and Troy Jackson Could Require Ranked-Choice Tabulation

The closely watched U.S. Senate race in Maine between Republican incumbent Susan Collins and Democratic challenger Troy Jackson may not be decided on Election Night. A New York Times, Portland Press Herald and Siena Research Institute poll found Jackson leading Collins by six percentage points among likely voters. Maine’s ranked-choice voting system could extend the count if no candidate receives more than half of the first-choice votes.

## Latest poll gives Jackson a six-point lead

The Portland Press Herald reported on November 1 that Jackson had the support of 51% of respondents, compared with 45% for Collins, in a survey of 714 likely Maine voters. The poll’s margin of error was 4.3 percentage points. The result represented a nine-point shift from the September version of the same survey, when Collins held a three-point advantage.

The poll reflects voter preferences during the survey period, not the final result. The race remains competitive, and polling estimates can differ from actual turnout and vote totals.

## Ranked-choice voting could extend the count

USA Today reported that Maine uses ranked-choice voting in statewide races with more than two candidates. In addition to Collins and Jackson, the Senate contest includes six declared write-in candidates. If no candidate receives more than 50% of first-choice votes, the count proceeds to ranked-choice tabulation.

Under the system, voters can rank candidates in order of preference. If no candidate reaches the required threshold in the initial count, the process uses voters’ later preferences to redistribute ballots as candidates are eliminated. The process continues until a candidate reaches the required level of support.

## The winner may not be known on Election Night

Maine Secretary of State Shenna Bellows told USA Today that a ranked-choice tabulation was highly possible because of the close polling and the number of write-in candidates. If the process is required, it could take several days to complete.

The timing matters because the initial vote count may not determine the final winner. The distribution of voters’ second and later preferences can affect the outcome after the first-choice count. The final result will depend on the official ballots and the state’s tabulation process.

## Economic concerns are central for voters

The Portland Press Herald reported that the economy was the top issue identified by respondents. Jackson had a slight advantage over Collins on trust in handling the cost of living, while Collins held a one-point edge on immigration. The poll also found Jackson with 52% support among unenrolled voters, compared with 41% for Collins.

Those figures describe the views of people surveyed and should not be treated as a forecast of the final vote. The race’s outcome will depend on who actually turns out and how voters rank candidates on their ballots.

## The race could affect control of the Senate

The contest has attracted national attention because its result could influence which party controls the Senate. Collins is a longtime Republican senator, while Jackson is the Democratic nominee. A polling lead does not establish that a seat has changed party control, and ranked-choice tabulation could alter the order of candidates after the initial count.

The immediate questions are whether one candidate can secure more than half of the first-choice vote and, if not, how voters’ later preferences will be distributed. The final outcome will be known only after Maine completes the required counting and certification.
