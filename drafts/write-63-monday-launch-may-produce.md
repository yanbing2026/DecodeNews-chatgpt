# SpaceX火箭发射原计划照亮美国西部夜空，“太空水母”观测受云层影响

The Weather Channel 11月2日报道，SpaceX 原计划于周一从加州 Vandenberg Space Force Base 发射 Falcon 9 火箭执行 USSF-423 任务，发射窗口从太平洋时间下午5时42分开始，可能在美国西部黄昏天空形成被称为“space jellyfish”的发光尾迹。火箭尾焰升至高空后仍能受到阳光照射，在地面已经变暗时形成明亮云状光影。不过，天气云层和发射计划调整可能影响观测。

## 黄昏发射形成发光尾迹

The Weather Channel 解释，火箭升空后，排气羽流进入高空，地面虽然已经进入黑暗，尾焰仍可能被太阳照亮，形成类似水母触须的发光形状。若天空条件合适，这种现象可能从数百英里外看到。类似的“space jellyfish”此前曾在一次 SpaceX 发射后出现。

## 原定发射窗口与地点

报道原定发射任务为 USSF-423，地点是加州 Vandenberg Space Force Base，四小时发射窗口从11月2日下午5时42分（太平洋标准时间）开始。该任务由 Falcon 9 执行，为美国太空军运送载荷。由于任务性质涉及国家安全，公开资料对载荷内容和任务细节有限。

## 不同地区观测条件不同

The Weather Channel 预测，犹他州南部、亚利桑那州和新墨西哥州在周末风暴过后可能出现较晴朗天空，观测条件较好。洛杉矶、拉斯维加斯和盐湖城部分地区可能有云，但仍可能存在观测窗口。旧金山、北加州和内华达州西部云量较多，观测条件较差；科罗拉多州当晚预计有降雨和高海拔降雪。

## 发射计划随后出现调整

发射日程网站在11月2日更新后显示，相关任务时间出现延期，公开排期对任务编号和新发射时间的显示也存在差异。部分资料将后续任务标为 USSF-432，而非天气报道中的 USSF-423。由于任务属于保密载荷，公开排期变化不一定会及时反映在所有媒体页面。不能将原定窗口视为最终实际发射时间。

## 观测现象取决于发射与天气

“太空水母”并非火箭专门释放的独立装置，而是火箭尾焰在特定高度和光照条件下形成的视觉效果。即使火箭按计划发射，云层也可能遮挡视线；若发射延期，原定观测时间也会改变。The Weather Channel 的文章属于发射前天气预报，不能证明现象已经出现。

目前可以确认的是，天气预报曾针对11月2日的发射窗口提供观测建议，但随后公开发射日程出现调整。实际是否发射、是否形成可见尾迹，以及各地能否观测到，需以发射方后续公告和当地天气为准。

# SpaceX Launch Was Expected to Create a “Space Jellyfish” Over the West, but Clouds and Schedule Changes Complicated Viewing

The Weather Channel reported on November 2 that a planned SpaceX Falcon 9 launch from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California could produce a glowing “space jellyfish” effect across parts of the western United States. The forecast was tied to a four-hour launch window scheduled to open at 5:42 p.m. Pacific Standard Time on Monday. Rocket exhaust can remain illuminated by sunlight high above the ground after twilight begins, creating a bright, spreading plume. Cloud cover and changes to the launch schedule could affect whether people saw the phenomenon.

## Twilight can illuminate the rocket plume

The Weather Channel explained that the rocket’s exhaust could catch direct sunlight after the ground had become dark. The illuminated plume can spread into a shape that observers compare with a jellyfish, with a bright body and trailing tendrils. Under favorable conditions, the effect can be visible from hundreds of miles away.

A similar phenomenon had been seen after an earlier SpaceX launch. The effect is not a separate object or a special payload; it is a visual result of the rocket’s exhaust, altitude, sunlight and viewing conditions.

## The original launch window and location

The Weather Channel’s forecast was based on a planned USSF-423 mission from Vandenberg Space Force Base. The four-hour window was scheduled to begin at 5:42 p.m. Pacific Standard Time on November 2. The mission was to use a Falcon 9 rocket to carry a payload for the U.S. Space Force.

Because the mission involves a national-security payload, public information about its contents and operational details was limited. The weather story focused on the timing and potential visibility of the launch rather than the classified payload.

## Viewing conditions varied across the West

The Weather Channel forecast the clearest conditions in southern Utah, Arizona and New Mexico, where storms from the previous weekend were expected to clear. Parts of Los Angeles, Las Vegas and Salt Lake City could have some cloud cover, but breaks in the clouds might still allow a view.

Northern California, including the San Francisco area, and western Nevada were forecast to have more cloud cover, making the phenomenon harder to see. Colorado was expected to have rain and high-elevation snow, reducing the chance of a clear view.

## The launch schedule subsequently changed

Public launch schedules updated around November 2 showed that the timing of the relevant mission had shifted. Some listings also used the designation USSF-432 rather than the USSF-423 mission name in the weather forecast. Because the mission is classified, public schedules can change and different sites may not update at the same time.

The original four-hour window should therefore not be treated as proof that the rocket launched at that time. The weather article was a pre-launch forecast, and later schedule changes mean that the actual launch timing must be checked against subsequent announcements.

## The visual effect depends on both launch and weather

A “space jellyfish” is a viewing phenomenon created when rocket exhaust remains sunlit at high altitude while observers on the ground are in darkness. Even if a rocket launches as scheduled, clouds can block the view. If the launch is delayed, the original viewing forecast may no longer apply.

The confirmed information is that The Weather Channel published a forecast for the planned November 2 window and identified regions with better or worse viewing conditions. The available reporting does not establish that the phenomenon was observed from every forecast region or that the mission lifted off during the original window. The actual launch status and visibility require confirmation from later launch updates and local weather reports.
