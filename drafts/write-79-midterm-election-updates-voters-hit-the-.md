美国中期选举投票日出现局部故障与天气干扰，官员呼吁选民继续投票

美国2026年中期选举于11月3日进入投票日。全国多地投票站陆续开放，国会控制权和多个州级职位成为焦点。当天出现的主要投票运行问题集中在纽约州Westchester County的选票扫描设备故障，以及佛罗里达州和美国西南部地区的暴雨、洪水造成的投票地点调整。地方选举官员表示，受影响地区仍可通过备用流程收取选票，并强调选民不应因设备或天气问题放弃投票。

## Westchester扫描设备无法读取选票

纽约州Westchester County多个投票站的扫描设备在清晨开放后出现故障，无法正常接收纸质选票。CBS New York报道，县内多数投票点受到影响，选民被要求继续填写纸质选票，再将选票放入安全存放箱。县政府和州选举部门正在协调处理，设备何时恢复正常当时尚不清楚。

Westchester County Board of Elections随后表示，问题与纸质选票厚度有关，扫描设备因此无法接收选票。ABC7 New York报道，选举工作人员安排使用橙色紧急选票袋暂存选票，并强调袋子将受到监控，之后按照选举程序计票。选民仍可在投票站填写并提交选票，不应因为扫描设备故障离开。

## 故障发生在竞争激烈的国会选区

Westchester County是纽约州第17国会选区的一部分，该选区共和党众议员Mike Lawler正面对民主党候选人Cait Conley的挑战。Axios指出，Westchester约占该选区三成选民，投票设备问题可能使该区计票工作更加复杂。两名候选人都公开呼吁支持者留在队伍中完成投票，并表示每张合资格选票都应被计算。

Times Union报道，县选举委员会两党成员共同呼吁选民保持耐心，并表示无法由扫描设备读取的选票会被安全保管，之后再行统计。纽约州总检察长Letitia James也表示其办公室正在关注事件。选票扫描故障可能拖慢计票，但现有报道没有证据表明选票因此丢失或被取消。

## 佛罗里达洪水迫使投票地点迁移

天气也给部分地区带来实际障碍。The Wall Street Journal报道，佛罗里达州东海岸洪水迫使选举官员在多个县迁移至少19个投票站，涉及Brevard、Broward、Highlands、Indian River、Martin、Okeechobee、Palm Beach和St. Lucie等县。部分受影响地区在2024年总统选举中支持Donald Trump，州级和国会选举的投票动员因此受到关注。

Forbes报道，佛罗里达州多个县因严重降雨和积水调整投票地点，官员要求选民出发前核对最新地点信息，并预留更多出行时间。投票地点迁移不等于选举取消，选举部门仍需确保选民能够在新的指定地点投票。

## 西南部风暴影响投票交通

CNN报道，造成美国西南部严重洪灾的风暴系统在投票日向美国中部移动，降雨可能影响从大平原到密西西比河流域的多个州。风暴此前曾短暂导致Phoenix附近投票地点关闭，并促使Utah和Colorado调整投票安排。CNN还报道，佛罗里达州另有超过20英寸的降雨，导致当地官员迁移18个投票地点。不同媒体报道的迁移数量和统计时间点可能不同，应以州和县选举部门最新公告为准。

## 备用流程和信息核验仍然重要

地方选举部门针对扫描设备故障采用纸票暂存等备用流程，天气影响地区则通过迁移投票地点维持投票。选民应查看本地选举部门发布的投票站地址和临时安排，而不是仅依赖社交媒体转发。设备故障、天气导致的地点调整和选票最终是否计入，是不同问题；目前地方官员公开表示受影响选票会依照程序保存和统计。

本次选举涉及国会两院和多州职位，部分选区竞争激烈。投票日的局部运行问题可能影响选民出行、排队和计票速度，但不能据此推断整体选举受到系统性干扰。最终结果仍需经过地方计票、核验和州级认证程序。

US Midterm Election Day Sees Local Equipment and Weather Disruptions as Officials Urge Voters to Continue

The 2026 US midterm elections reached Election Day on November 3, with polling places opening across the country as control of Congress and several state offices were at stake. The main operational disruptions reported during the day involved ballot-scanning problems in Westchester County, New York, and heavy rain and flooding that forced changes to polling locations in Florida and parts of the Southwest. Local election officials said voters in affected areas could still cast ballots through backup procedures and urged them not to abandon the process because of equipment or weather problems.

## Westchester scanners reject paper ballots

Voting scanners at polling locations across Westchester County began experiencing problems after polls opened in the morning. The machines were not accepting paper ballots. CBS New York reported that most polling sites in the county were affected and that voters were being instructed to complete paper ballots and place them in secure containers. County and state election officials were coordinating a response, but the timing for a full restoration of the scanners was not initially clear.

The Westchester County Board of Elections later said the issue involved the thickness of the paper ballots, which prevented the scanners from accepting them. ABC7 New York reported that election workers were using orange emergency ballot bags to hold the completed ballots. Officials said the bags would remain secured and monitored, and that the ballots would be counted under election procedures. Voters could continue to fill out and submit ballots at their polling places and were told not to leave because of the scanner problem.

## The disruption affects a competitive House district

Westchester County is part of New York's 17th Congressional District, where Republican Representative Mike Lawler is facing Democratic challenger Cait Conley. Axios noted that Westchester accounts for roughly 30% of the district, making the scanner problem a potential complication for one of the closely watched House contests. Both candidates publicly urged voters to remain in line and cast their ballots, while saying that every eligible vote should be counted.

The Times Union reported that the county Board of Elections, whose members include both parties, urged voters to remain patient. Ballots that could not be scanned would be kept securely and tabulated later. New York Attorney General Letitia James also said her office was monitoring the situation. The scanner malfunction could slow the counting process, but the reports available at the time did not establish that ballots had been lost or invalidated.

## Florida flooding forces polling-place relocations

Weather created practical obstacles elsewhere. The Wall Street Journal reported that flooding along Florida's eastern coast forced election officials to relocate at least 19 polling stations across several counties: Brevard, Broward, Highlands, Indian River, Martin, Okeechobee, Palm Beach and St. Lucie. Several of the affected counties had supported Donald Trump in the 2024 presidential election, adding attention to how the changes might affect turnout in state and congressional contests.

Forbes reported that heavy rain and flooding led officials in multiple Florida counties to change polling locations. Voters were urged to check the latest location information before traveling and to allow extra time. Relocating a polling place does not mean that an election has been cancelled. Election officials remain responsible for ensuring that eligible voters can cast ballots at the designated replacement locations.

## Storms disrupt travel in the Southwest and central US

CNN reported that a storm system responsible for severe flooding in the Southwest was moving east across parts of the central United States on Election Day. Rain could affect areas from the Great Plains to the Mississippi Valley. The storm had earlier forced a temporary closure of voting sites near Phoenix and prompted changes to voting arrangements in Utah and Colorado. CNN also reported that more than 20 inches of rain in parts of Florida had led officials to relocate 18 polling places. Counts of affected locations can differ by publication and update time, so the latest state and county election notices remain the most reliable source for voters.

## Backup procedures and accurate information remain essential

Local election officials responded to the scanner problem by using secure ballot storage and later tabulation. In areas affected by severe weather, authorities used polling-place relocations to keep voting available. Voters should check the websites or official notices of their local election offices for current addresses and temporary arrangements rather than relying only on social-media posts. Equipment failures, weather-related location changes and whether a ballot is ultimately counted are separate questions. Local officials said the affected ballots would be secured and counted according to established procedures.

The election includes contests for both chambers of Congress and offices in multiple states, with several races expected to be close. Local disruptions can affect travel, waiting times and the speed of vote tabulation, but they do not by themselves establish that the election as a whole has been compromised. Final outcomes still depend on local counting, verification and state certification procedures.
