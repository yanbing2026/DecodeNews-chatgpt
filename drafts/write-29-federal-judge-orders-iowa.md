# 联邦法官要求爱荷华州允许撒旦教会在州议会大厦举办节日活动

一名联邦法官裁定，爱荷华州官员既然允许其他宗教团体在州议会大厦展示宗教内容并举办节日活动，就必须向 The Satanic Temple of Iowa 提供同等申请机会。法院发布初步禁令，暂时要求州政府不得以宗教立场为由区别对待该团体。案件仍未结束，法院之后还将决定是否发布永久禁令。

## 法院要求平等对待宗教团体

Radio Iowa 11月2日报道，联邦法院认为，州政府不能一方面批准其他宗教团体在 Iowa Capitol 举办活动，另一方面拒绝 The Satanic Temple of Iowa 的类似申请。该组织希望在州议会大厦举行节日活动，州官员此前连续两年拒绝其申请。法官的初步禁令要求州政府在许可处理上遵守平等原则，而不是根据申请者的宗教身份决定是否开放公共场地。

## 争议延续自此前展示活动

The Satanic Temple 曾在2023年于 Iowa Capitol Rotunda 设置展示物。Radio Iowa 报道称，该展示后来遭到破坏，一名来自 Mississippi 的男子在看到相关计划后驾车前往爱荷华州。此后，该组织继续申请举办节日活动，但州政府在过去两年均拒绝批准。组织方面认为，其他宗教团体获准使用相同场地，政府却拒绝其申请，构成了对宗教表达的差别待遇。

## ACLU提起诉讼并申请禁令

ACLU of Iowa 于8月提起诉讼，主张州政府的拒绝行为侵犯了该组织的宪法权利。该组织的律师在新闻发布会上表示，政府不能偏袒某一种宗教，也不能因为某个宗教团体的信仰不受欢迎而对其区别对待。法院此次发布的是 preliminary injunction，即初步禁令，并非案件最终判决。它在诉讼继续期间为申请方提供临时司法保护。

## 永久禁令仍待法院决定

Radio Iowa 援引 ACLU of Iowa 法律负责人 Rita Bettis Austen 的说法称，初步禁令旨在纠正此前的许可处理方式，让申请方能够像其他团体一样使用公共空间。法院下一步仍需审理是否应发布 permanent injunction。若最终发布永久禁令，州政府可能需要长期调整相关许可做法；如果没有，当前临时措施的后续效力则取决于法院进一步命令。

## 公共场地许可与政府中立

这起案件的核心并不是法院认可或否定 The Satanic Temple 的宗教主张，而是政府管理公共场地时是否保持中立。美国宪法保护宗教表达，也限制政府对宗教团体进行不平等对待。政府可以制定适用于所有申请人的时间、场地、安全和秩序规则，但若允许其他宗教团体举办活动，却仅因申请团体身份而拒绝，便可能触及宪法问题。

目前，已知裁定只涉及初步禁令。Radio Iowa 报道没有列出法院对未来活动的具体日期、许可细节或最终赔偿安排，也没有说明永久禁令何时作出。案件仍在联邦法院审理，州政府后续如何执行以及法院最终如何裁决，仍有待观察。

# Federal Judge Orders Iowa to Allow Satanic Temple to Hold a Holiday Event at the State Capitol

A federal judge has ruled that Iowa officials must give the Satanic Temple of Iowa the same opportunity to obtain a permit for a holiday event at the State Capitol that has been made available to other religious groups. The court issued a preliminary injunction after the organization challenged the state’s repeated denials of its requests. The order is an interim ruling, not a final judgment in the case. The federal court is expected to consider whether a permanent injunction should follow.

## Court requires equal treatment of religious groups

Radio Iowa reported on November 2 that the federal judge concluded state officials who approve permits for religious displays and holiday events at the Iowa Capitol must also provide a permit to the Satanic Temple of Iowa. The group had sought permission to hold holiday events at the Capitol, but state officials had denied its requests for the previous two years.

The ruling centers on the state’s treatment of applicants using a public venue. It does not require officials to endorse the group’s beliefs. Instead, it addresses whether the government can allow comparable religious activity by some groups while denying access to another group because of its religious identity or message.

## Dispute follows an earlier Capitol display

The organization previously installed a display in the Iowa Capitol Rotunda in 2023. Radio Iowa reported that the display was vandalized by a man from Mississippi who drove to Iowa after reading about the group’s plans. The organization later sought permission to hold holiday events, but officials rejected its requests over the next two years.

The repeated denials led the group to argue that it was being treated differently from other religious organizations that had been allowed to use the same public space. The dispute therefore involves both access to a government venue and the constitutional limits on how officials may administer a permit system.

## ACLU of Iowa brought the lawsuit

The ACLU of Iowa filed a lawsuit in August, arguing that the denials violated the organization’s constitutional rights. During a news conference, senior staff attorney Shafali Aurora said the government cannot favor one religion over another or discriminate against religious groups because officials disagree with their beliefs. Legal director Rita Bettis Austen described the preliminary injunction as a correction to the way the state had handled the group’s requests.

The court’s action is a preliminary injunction. That distinction is important: it provides temporary relief while the litigation continues, but it does not settle every legal question in the case. The available report does not say that the court has issued a final ruling on all claims or determined what permanent remedy will apply.

## Permanent relief remains undecided

Radio Iowa said the U.S. District Court will next consider whether to issue a permanent injunction. A permanent order could require Iowa officials to maintain a permit process that treats the group on the same terms as other applicants. Until that decision is made, the preliminary injunction governs the immediate dispute, subject to any further court orders.

The report does not provide a confirmed date for the next ruling, the precise schedule for the requested holiday event, or details of any final damages. Those issues should not be assumed from the preliminary order.

## Government neutrality is the central question

The case is not a judicial endorsement of the Satanic Temple’s religious position. It concerns the constitutional principle that government officials must remain neutral when administering access to public facilities. A state may impose neutral rules covering scheduling, safety, space and public order. But a permit system can raise constitutional concerns if comparable groups are treated differently because of their religious identity or viewpoint.

The distinction between neutral administration and viewpoint-based exclusion is central to the dispute. Iowa officials will have to respond to the court’s interim order as the case proceeds. The eventual outcome may clarify how the state handles religious displays and holiday events at the Capitol, but the November 2 ruling itself remains preliminary.

For now, the established result is limited: a federal judge ordered equal treatment at the permit stage and issued preliminary relief. Whether that protection becomes permanent remains for the court to decide.
