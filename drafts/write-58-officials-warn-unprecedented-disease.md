# Africa CDC警告埃博拉疫情扩散前所未有，呼吁各国提高防备

Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention（Africa CDC）负责人 Jean Kaseya 警告，非洲当前埃博拉疫情的地理扩散范围前所未有，若各国不提高防备，病毒可能传播至非洲以外地区。The Guardian 10月28日报道，Kaseya 将疫情与新冠早期扩散作比较，呼吁各国增加准备和治疗能力。该警告发布时，疫情已累计超过10,000例病例，死亡人数接近5,000人。

## 疫情扩散到多个非洲地区

Kaseya 表示，这是埃博拉首次影响非洲三个地区，地理扩散程度前所未见。他警告，各国不能认为自己安全，必须提高准备水平，并考虑治疗能力和防控措施。The Guardian 报道称，疫情主要集中在刚果民主共和国，但病例已在乌干达、肯尼亚和其他地区出现。

## 官方担忧跨境传播扩大

Kaseya 将当前情况与新冠疫情早期阶段作比较，指出当时许多国家最初认为疫情局限于某一地区，之后病毒迅速扩散。他强调，埃博拉疫情同样可能跨境传播，因此需要国际合作、病例监测和边境防控。该表态是公共卫生风险警告，并不表示病毒已经在英国或其他欧洲国家广泛传播。

## 疫情应对需要更多资金和能力

The Guardian 报道称，Kaseya 呼吁增加资金，以提高各国准备水平和可能的治疗能力。当前疫情由 Bundibugyo 病毒株引起，该病毒株缺乏获批疫苗和特效治疗。各国需要加强病例发现、接触者追踪、感染控制和医疗资源部署。冲突和卫生系统资源不足，也会使部分受影响地区更难开展防疫工作。

## 国际防备不等于已经发生跨洲暴发

Kaseya 的警告强调潜在风险，而不是确认疫情已经扩散到欧洲。The Guardian 报道时提到，非洲 CDC 负责人呼吁各大洲提高准备，但并未称英国已出现本轮疫情病例。后续媒体报道显示，疫情在刚果（金）及周边国家持续发展，但各国病例情况和统计口径需要分别看待。

## 疫情数字随报告日期变化

The Guardian 在10月28日的报道中称，病例已超过10,000例，死亡人数接近5,000人。之后 Africa CDC 在11月初公布的区域数据进一步上升。不同时间点的数据不能混为同一统计快照。当前警告的核心是防止跨境传播和提高准备，而不是用单一死亡数字判断所有国家的风险。

# Africa CDC Warns of Unprecedented Ebola Spread and Urges Countries to Prepare

Jean Kaseya, head of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, has warned that the geographic spread of the current Ebola outbreak is unprecedented and that countries should increase their preparedness. In an interview reported by The Guardian on October 28, Kaseya said governments should not assume they are safe if they fail to take appropriate measures. He compared the risk of wider spread with the early stages of the COVID-19 pandemic and called for more funding to strengthen preparedness and treatment capacity.

## The outbreak has reached multiple African regions

Kaseya said the current outbreak was the first time Ebola had affected three African regions, describing its geographic expansion as unprecedented. The Guardian reported that the Democratic Republic of the Congo remained the center of the outbreak, while cases had also been reported in Uganda, Kenya and other locations.

The warning reflects concern about cross-border transmission. It does not mean that the virus has already spread widely to the United Kingdom or other European countries. Kaseya was urging governments to prepare for a possible expansion rather than reporting a confirmed outbreak in those countries.

## Officials are concerned about wider transmission

Kaseya compared the situation with the early stages of COVID-19, when many countries initially believed the outbreak was geographically limited before it spread internationally. He said countries across all regions should increase their preparedness and consider the resources needed to identify and treat cases.

The warning highlights the importance of surveillance, rapid detection and coordination across borders. Countries that are not currently reporting cases may still need to review their preparedness plans, particularly if travel links connect them to affected areas.

## Preparedness requires funding and capacity

The Guardian reported that Kaseya called for additional funding to improve preparedness and possible treatment capacity. The current outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola, for which there is no approved vaccine or specific treatment. Public health responses therefore rely on identifying cases, tracing contacts, isolating patients, protecting health workers and providing supportive care.

Conflict and limited health-system resources can make these tasks more difficult in affected areas. The ability to reach communities and maintain public trust is also important. Kaseya’s warning was directed at the need to strengthen those systems before cases spread further.

## A warning does not mean a cross-continental outbreak has occurred

Kaseya’s statement described a risk that other countries should prepare for. The Guardian did not report that the current outbreak had spread to the United Kingdom. The distinction between a warning and a confirmed case is important when communicating public-health developments, especially when the disease is receiving international attention.

Later reports in early November showed that the outbreak continued to affect the DRC and neighboring countries. The number of cases and deaths changed as new data were published, and different agencies used different reporting cutoffs. Those later figures should not be retroactively attributed to the October 28 warning.

## Outbreak figures depend on the reporting date

At the time of The Guardian’s October 28 report, the outbreak had exceeded 10,000 cases and was approaching 5,000 deaths. Africa CDC published higher regional totals in early November. The figures describe different reporting dates and should not be treated as one simultaneous count.

The central message from Kaseya was that countries should strengthen preparedness before additional cross-border spread occurs. The warning is about readiness, funding and international coordination, not proof that every country is already experiencing transmission.
