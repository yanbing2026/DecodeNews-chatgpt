# ICE新招募人员遭刑事起诉，扩编审查与培训问题再受关注

## 追车事件进入刑事程序
美国得克萨斯州哈里斯县一名ICE执法人员因涉嫌驾车撞击一名正在逃跑的男子，被大陪审团以持致命武器严重袭击罪起诉。Mario Alberto Morales于11月2日向警方自首并出庭，法官将保释金定为3万美元。案件发生在特朗普政府扩大移民执法队伍之际，也让新招募人员的背景审查、培训和执法规范再次受到关注。

## 检方与ICE对经过说法相反
检方称，10月10日，Morales在休斯敦一处停车场追逐Miguel Parra Hernandez，随后驾驶SUV撞上这名墨西哥建筑工人。哈里斯县官员表示，监控和执法记录仪视频与ICE关于Hernandez跑到车辆前方的说法不一致。Hernandez腿部骨折，正在康复机构接受治疗，并处于ICE监管之下。ICE否认撞击是故意行为，称相关指控不实。

## 被告否认犯罪并获准保释
Morales的律师称，车辆碰撞是意外，指控属于政治性检控。大陪审团起诉只是刑事程序的开始，并不代表被告已经被定罪。法官将保释金定为3万美元，低于检方要求的7.5万美元。律师称Morales在ICE任职略多于一年，曾在军队服役，也有执法相关工作经历。

## 过往任职记录受到审视
NBC News报道，Morales于2025年8月加入ICE。此前，他曾在得州惩教系统工作，并在仍处于试用期时离职。得州惩教部门发言人称，其人事记录涉及虚报安全巡查记录，即将未执行的巡查登记为已完成。Morales的律师则强调其服役和公共服务经历，并指责地方检察官借案件攻击联邦移民执法政策。过往任职记录的报道不等于本案指控已经得到证明。

## ICE快速扩编引发更广泛问题
本案发生在联邦政府大幅增加移民执法人员之后。Associated Press此前调查指出，ICE在扩编过程中录用大量新人员，部分录用流程曾在背景审查尚未完成时推进。NBC News也报道，部分新招募人员的资历和训练标准受到内部人员质疑，ICE随后表示将对接受加速培训的人员进行再培训。这些报道反映机构层面的审查问题，不能据此推断所有新招募人员都不合格。

## 司法责任仍待调查和审理
哈里斯县检察官认为，视频证据支持对Morales提出刑事指控；被告律师和ICE则否认其故意撞人，并质疑地方政府调查联邦执法人员的权限。案件后续可能涉及联邦执法人员履职期间是否受联邦法律保护等争议。现阶段可以确认的是大陪审团已提出指控、被告已获保释；是否构成犯罪、执法是否违反规定，以及招聘和培训制度是否存在系统性缺陷，仍需分别调查和审理。

# Indicted ICE Officer’s Case Puts Hiring Surge and Training Under Scrutiny

## A vehicle pursuit becomes a criminal case
An Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer in Harris County, Texas, has been indicted on a charge of aggravated assault with a deadly weapon after prosecutors accused him of striking a man with an SUV during a pursuit. Mario Alberto Morales surrendered and appeared in court on November 2. A judge set bond at $30,000. The case has drawn attention to the vetting and training of recently recruited immigration officers as the Trump administration continues to expand federal immigration enforcement.

## Prosecutors and ICE dispute what happened
Prosecutors say Morales pursued Miguel Parra Hernandez through a Houston parking lot on October 10 and struck the Mexican construction worker. Harris County officials say surveillance and body-camera footage contradict ICE’s account that Hernandez ran in front of the vehicle. Hernandez suffered a broken leg and is recovering at a rehabilitation facility while in ICE custody. ICE has denied that the collision was intentional and described the allegations as false. Those competing accounts have not been resolved at trial.

## The officer denies wrongdoing and is released on bond
Morales’ attorney says the collision was accidental and has characterized the prosecution as politically motivated. An indictment is an accusation, not a finding of guilt. At the November 2 hearing, the judge set bond at $30,000, below the $75,000 prosecutors had requested. Morales’ attorney has said his client had worked for ICE for a little more than a year and had previously served in the military and held law-enforcement-related jobs. The criminal case will determine whether prosecutors can prove the charge.

## Previous employment records come under review
NBC News reported that Morales joined ICE in August 2025. Before that, he worked for the Texas Department of Criminal Justice and resigned while on probation. A department spokesperson said his employment record involved falsifying security-round records by documenting checks that had not been conducted. Morales’ lawyer emphasized his military and public-service background and accused the district attorney of using the case to attack the federal government’s immigration agenda. The reported employment history is relevant to screening questions, but it does not establish whether Morales committed the alleged assault.

## The agency’s rapid expansion raises broader questions
The case comes after a major expansion of the federal immigration workforce. An Associated Press investigation published earlier in 2026 reported that ICE hired thousands of officers and that some recruits were allowed to begin work before all background checks were complete. NBC News has also reported concerns from officials about the qualifications and training of some new hires. ICE has said it would retrain thousands of officers who received expedited instruction during the hiring surge. These reports raise questions about institutional safeguards; they do not show that every recent recruit is unqualified or that the hiring process caused this incident.

## Legal responsibility and policy questions remain open
Harris County prosecutors argue that video evidence supports the charge against Morales. His attorney and ICE dispute the allegation that he deliberately struck Hernandez and have questioned the authority of local officials to investigate a federal officer acting in an official capacity. The case could involve legal arguments about federal protections for officers carrying out their duties. At this stage, the established procedural facts are that a grand jury returned an indictment and Morales was released on bond. His guilt, the legality of the encounter and any broader failures in recruitment or training remain separate questions for investigation and the courts.
