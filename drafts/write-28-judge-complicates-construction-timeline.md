# 法官为特朗普纪念拱门项目设置多重通知期限，施工时间表受限

美国联邦法官 Tanya Chutkan 11月2日下令，特朗普政府必须在纪念拱门项目的关键行政决定作出后迅速通知法院，并对任何施工准备活动提前通报。该命令增加了多个通知节点，使政府原定最早于11月5日启动施工的计划受到限制。法院并未在这次命令中最终裁定项目是否合法，而是在此前诉讼命令基础上加强监督。

## 行政决定须及时通知法院

美联社记者 Gary Fields 报道，Chutkan 要求政府在 National Capital Planning Commission、National Park Service 和 Interior Department 作出相关决定后，分别在一小时内通知法院。政府此前向法院说明，项目还需要完成规划委员会批准、内政部最终授权以及正式公告等程序。行政部门的通知称，相关程序最早可能在11月5日完成，施工也可能从当天开始。

## 施工准备也受提前通知约束

法院命令不仅涉及正式开工，也涉及与拱门施工有关的活动或准备工作。美联社转述法官命令称，被告必须在任何与拱门施工有关或为施工做准备的活动发生前30分钟通知法院。法官同时提醒政府，在 National Park Service 发布最终授权之前，或未提前14天通知的情况下进行施工或拆除，都可能违反法院此前的命令。

## 诉讼挑战项目授权程序

该案由反对项目的原告提起，诉讼质疑政府是否有权在 Memorial Circle 推进建设。美联社此前报道，原告包括三名退伍军人和一名退休建筑历史学家，他们请求法院在诉讼期间阻止政府及 National Park Service 推进建设，理由涉及国会授权问题。11月1日，District of Columbia 和 Virginia 也就拱门项目提起诉讼，法院将相关诉讼合并处理。

## 最早开工日期并非施工许可

政府此前表示，如果规划批准、内政部最终决定和公告等环节完成，施工可能在11月5日开始。但法官要求及时报告每项决定，并重申此前关于14天通知的要求，意味着政府不能把某个行政审批节点视为自动开工许可。任何施工或拆除行为都必须符合现有法院命令和相关行政程序。

## 法院尚未作出实体裁决

这次命令主要规定信息披露和时间要求，并没有最终判定纪念拱门是否可以建设，也没有就国会是否必须授权作出最终结论。诉讼仍在进行，法院可能根据政府后续通知和原告主张进一步处理。当前能够确认的是，法官要求政府对审批与施工准备过程提供更密集的即时通知，项目时间表因此受到程序性约束。

# Judge Sets Multiple Notice Deadlines for Trump’s Memorial Arch, Complicating Construction Timeline

A federal judge has imposed additional notice requirements on the Trump administration as it seeks to move forward with a proposed memorial arch in Washington. U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan’s November 2 order requires the government to notify the court quickly after key administrative decisions and to provide advance notice of activities connected to construction. The order complicates the administration’s stated plan to begin work as early as November 5, but it does not finally decide whether the project may be built.

## Administrative decisions must be reported promptly

The Associated Press reported that Chutkan requires the government to notify the court within one hour of relevant decisions by the National Capital Planning Commission, the National Park Service and the Department of the Interior. The administration had previously told the court that the project’s path to construction involved several steps: approval by the planning commission, a final decision by the Interior Department authorizing construction, and publication of the required notice.

The government’s filing said the National Park Service could publish its notice as early as November 5, 2026, and that construction could begin as early as that date. The judge’s order means the court must be informed as those decisions occur, rather than learning about them only after the process has advanced.

## Construction preparation also requires notice

The order covers more than the final decision to begin building. According to the AP account, Chutkan directed the defendants to provide notice within 30 minutes of any activity related to or in preparation for construction of the arch on Memorial Circle.

The judge also reminded the administration that construction or demolition before the National Park Service issues its final agency action, or without 14 days’ notice, would violate the court’s earlier orders. The distinction matters because preparatory work can affect the site even before the government describes a project as formally underway. The new reporting requirements are intended to let the court monitor those steps while the legal challenge remains active.

## Lawsuits challenge the project’s authorization

The litigation concerns the administration’s authority to proceed with the proposed structure at Memorial Circle. An earlier AP report said the original plaintiffs included three veterans and a retired architectural historian. They asked the court to stop the administration and the National Park Service from taking steps to construct a structure at the site without congressional authorization while the case is pending.

The legal challenges expanded on November 1, when the District of Columbia and Virginia filed a separate lawsuit. AP reported that Chutkan consolidated that case with the original litigation and ordered a joint briefing schedule. The claims raise questions about the approvals and authority required for a project at the location; they are allegations being litigated, not final judicial findings.

## November 5 is a possible date, not an unconditional start

The administration’s notice identified November 5 as the earliest possible date for the next approval and for construction to commence. But that date depends on the required administrative steps and compliance with the court’s existing orders. The new notice deadlines do not themselves grant permission to start work. They require the government to keep the court informed and preserve the notice protections already imposed.

The order therefore adds procedural oversight to a project that was already facing legal opposition. Whether the administration can satisfy the required steps, and whether the court will allow construction to proceed while the case continues, remain separate questions.

## The court has not issued a final ruling on the arch

Chutkan’s November 2 order addresses notice and supervision. It does not finally determine whether the memorial arch is lawful, whether Congress must authorize it, or whether the plaintiffs will ultimately prevail. Those issues remain part of the pending litigation.

For now, the immediate effect is clear: the administration must report key agency decisions within an hour, provide notice within 30 minutes of construction-related or preparatory activity, and comply with the earlier 14-day notice requirement. The project’s timetable is consequently subject to closer judicial monitoring, and November 5 remains only the earliest date identified in the government’s filing, not a guaranteed construction start.
