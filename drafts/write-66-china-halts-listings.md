# 中国据报暂缓IPO和大股东减持，以稳定持续承压的股市

## 监管部门据报向投行发出口头指引
中国证券监管部门据报已要求投资银行暂缓新的首次公开募股（IPO），并暂时限制现有股东大规模出售股份，以缓解股市下跌压力。Financial Times援引三名了解讨论情况的人士称，中国证监会近期与投资银行举行会议，发出了非正式的“窗口指导”。这并非已经公开发布的正式禁令；Reuters随后报道了这一消息，同时明确表示无法独立核实该报道。中国金融监管部门没有立即回应Reuters的置评请求。因此，现阶段更准确的表述是监管层据报要求暂缓相关交易，而不是已确认实施全面、正式的IPO禁令。

## 市场下跌令融资活动承压
这一消息出现之际，中国股市经历了明显回落。Financial Times和Reuters报道，沪深300指数在10月下跌8.5%，创十多年来最差单月表现。FT还称，该指数年内跌幅达到15%，而中国今年IPO融资规模已超过人民币2920亿元，是2023年以来最高水平。暂停新股发行可能减少新股供给对存量市场资金的分流，但也可能打断IPO市场刚刚恢复的融资节奏。FT报道提到，存储芯片企业长江存储原本被视为可能上市的公司之一，监管指导可能令相关计划延后。

## 与回购和流动性支持措施同步推出
IPO暂缓的报道，与监管部门近期推出的稳市工具同时出现。Reuters、Bloomberg和City News Service报道，中国人民银行与金融监管部门重新启用股票市场支持工具，包括证券、基金、保险公司互换便利，以及为上市公司回购股份提供融资的再贷款安排。Business Insider报道称，参与申请流动性支持的证券公司和基金管理机构达到31家，申请规模超过人民币1000亿元。央行还将股票回购再贷款利率从1.25%下调至1%。这些措施旨在为机构买入股票和上市公司回购提供资金，但并不意味着监管部门能够直接扭转市场情绪。

## 投资者对政策效果仍有疑虑
市场反应显示，政策支持尚未带来持续反弹。Reuters报道，相关措施公布后，科技股仍然承压，沪深300和上证综指在周二早盘一度下跌超过1%，之后才收窄跌幅。Bloomberg经The Edge刊出的报道也指出，科技股集中的科创50指数周二下跌近4%，前一交易日因政策预期产生的涨幅被抹去。Barron's则提醒，股市疲弱可能拖慢IPO市场，并限制科技企业通过公开市场融资的能力。CNN此前的报道将市场压力与消费疲弱、人工智能投资疑虑及房地产低迷联系起来。

## 暂缓发行无法替代基本面改善
分析人士认为，限制股票供给和增加回购资金，或许能缓和短期抛售压力，但并不能自动改善企业盈利、居民消费或房地产市场。Business Insider援引研究机构观点称，当前措施更像是为市场托底，而不是启动新一轮全面上涨。Reuters Breakingviews也指出，股市能否持续恢复，最终仍取决于企业盈利和更广泛的经济政策。与此同时，IPO暂缓若持续较长时间，可能影响企业融资、投资银行承销业务和科技行业扩张。由于目前有关暂停IPO的消息主要来自匿名人士和媒体报道，措施范围、持续时间及是否覆盖其他股权融资方式仍有待监管部门进一步说明。

# China Reportedly Pauses IPOs and Major Share Sales to Support Its Struggling Stock Market

## Regulators reportedly issue informal guidance to banks
China’s securities regulator has reportedly told investment banks to pause new initial public offerings and temporarily restrict large share sales by existing shareholders as authorities seek to ease pressure on the country’s stock market. The Financial Times, citing three people familiar with the discussions, reported that the China Securities Regulatory Commission had held meetings with investment banks and issued informal “window guidance.” The reported instruction is not the same as a publicly announced formal ban. Reuters later reported the development but said it could not independently verify the Financial Times account. Chinese financial regulators did not immediately respond to Reuters’ request for comment. For now, the most accurate description is that regulators have reportedly asked banks to hold off on certain transactions, rather than that a formal nationwide IPO prohibition has been confirmed.

## A market downturn is putting fundraising under pressure
The report emerged after a sharp decline in Chinese equities. The Financial Times and Reuters said the CSI 300 index fell 8.5% in October, its worst monthly performance in more than a decade. The FT also reported that the index was down 15% for the year, while IPO fundraising in China had exceeded 292 billion yuan, the highest level since 2023. A pause in new listings could reduce the amount of capital diverted from existing shares into newly issued stock. But it could also interrupt an IPO market that had recently regained momentum. The FT cited memory-chip maker Yangtze Memory Technologies Corp. as a company that had been expected to list, suggesting that the reported guidance could delay plans for some large issuers.

## The reported pause accompanies other market-support measures
The IPO report came as regulators were introducing additional tools to support equities. Reuters, Bloomberg and City News Service reported that the People’s Bank of China and financial regulators had revived market-support facilities, including a swap program for securities firms, funds and insurers, as well as a relending program that helps finance share buybacks by listed companies. Business Insider reported that 31 securities firms and fund managers had applied for more than 100 billion yuan in liquidity support. The central bank also reduced the interest rate on its share-buyback relending facility from 1.25% to 1%. These measures are intended to make funding available for institutional stock purchases and corporate buybacks. They do not, however, guarantee a change in investor sentiment.

## Investors remain unconvinced about the impact
Market trading suggested that the policy support had not produced a sustained rebound. Reuters reported that technology shares remained under pressure after the measures were announced. The CSI 300 and Shanghai Composite indexes were down more than 1% early on Tuesday before trimming their losses. A Bloomberg report published by The Edge said the technology-heavy STAR 50 index fell nearly 4% on Tuesday, erasing gains made in the previous session on expectations of policy support. Barron’s warned that the downturn could slow China’s IPO market and limit the ability of technology companies to raise capital through public listings. Earlier reporting by CNN linked the broader market weakness to soft consumer spending, concerns about artificial-intelligence investment and the prolonged property downturn.

## A listing pause cannot replace stronger fundamentals
Analysts say restricting the supply of shares and making buyback funding easier may help limit near-term selling pressure, but neither measure automatically improves corporate earnings, household consumption or the property market. Business Insider cited research analysts who described the latest measures as an attempt to put a floor under markets rather than trigger another broad rally. Reuters Breakingviews also argued that a lasting recovery will depend on better corporate earnings and broader economic policy. If the reported IPO pause lasts, it could affect companies seeking capital, investment banks that underwrite listings and technology firms planning expansion. Because the reported suspension is based largely on anonymous sources and media accounts, its scope, duration and application to other forms of equity financing remain unclear pending further clarification from regulators.
