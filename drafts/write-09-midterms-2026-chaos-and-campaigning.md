# 美国中期选举前夕，投票干扰与联邦介入风险引发关注

## 选举前的制度压力升高
美国选民即将参加2026年中期选举，投票权组织和地方选举官员正密切关注可能影响投票的多种风险。ProPublica调查报道梳理了五类问题：联邦执法人员是否介入投票现场、针对疑似非公民选民的审查是否误伤合法选民、投票规则和设备是否造成障碍、选举安全是否受到威胁，以及投票结束后是否出现挑战结果的行动。报道指出，法院此前已阻止特朗普政府多项扩大联邦选举控制权的做法，但选举官员仍担心持续施压影响公众信任。

## 联邦执法人员是否出现在投票点
报道举例称，佛罗里达州Leon County一处提前投票地点外曾出现两辆带有标识的海关与边境保护局车辆。当地选举主管Mark Earley最初担心联邦执法人员可能在投票点执行任务，后来得知相关人员只是停留并准备投票。DHS向ProPublica表示，ICE没有计划在投票地点开展执法行动，只有在出现安全威胁时才可能采取针对性行动。报道同时指出，联邦法律对执法人员在投票地点附近的活动有限制。

## 疑似非公民名单可能误伤选民
特朗普政府针对疑似非公民选民的行动引起选民资格争议。司法部曾要求加州不要计算其声称“可能”为非公民的两万多名选民的邮寄选票。加州称相关名单不可靠；洛杉矶县抽样分析发现，名单中的一些人并未在该县登记或从未投过票，也有人在登记表上注明自己出生于美国。华盛顿州官员也质疑联邦提供的400人名单，称其中包括美国公民、未登记者和重复记录。

## 投票规则和安全问题并存
ProPublica还关注州级投票规则变化可能造成的障碍。报道提到，北卡罗来纳州邮寄选票的新要求增加了信封密封步骤，选民若遗漏其中一个步骤，选票可能被拒收。得州部分县减少投票地点，也可能加剧排队和混乱。与此同时，地方官员担心网络攻击、实体威胁和针对计票地点的虚假警报；报道回顾了2024年虚假炸弹威胁曾导致多地短暂疏散。

## 选后法律挑战可能延续
选举律师还担心，政府在投票前的行动可能预示选后会出现更积极的法律挑战。司法部曾起诉阿拉斯加，试图在共和党现任参议员Dan Sullivan落败时阻止州方认证结果，理由是另一名同名候选人可能造成选民混淆。联邦法官已驳回该诉讼。ProPublica指出，联邦政府介入正在进行的选举程序不同于候选人通常在投票后向州法院提出的争议。选举能否顺利完成，仍取决于地方官员依法管理投票和计票，以及各方是否尊重司法程序。

# Election Officials Watch for Disruption and Federal Interference Ahead of the Midterms

## Pressure on election systems is rising
As Americans prepared to vote in the 2026 midterm elections, local election officials and voting-rights groups were watching several potential sources of disruption. A ProPublica investigation outlined five areas of concern: possible federal law-enforcement activity near polling places, challenges to voters identified as potential noncitizens, changes to voting procedures and equipment, physical and cyber threats, and legal challenges after ballots are cast. The report said courts had blocked many of the Trump administration’s efforts to expand federal control over elections, but officials remained concerned that continued pressure could weaken public confidence.

## Could federal agents appear at polling places?
ProPublica described an episode in Leon County, Florida, where two marked Customs and Border Protection vehicles parked outside a busy early-voting location. County election supervisor Mark Earley initially feared federal agents might be conducting enforcement activity near voters. He later learned that the agents had stopped to vote themselves while traveling out of state. The Department of Homeland Security told ProPublica that ICE was not planning operations targeting polling locations and said enforcement at a polling place would be limited to a safety threat. The article also noted legal restrictions on federal law-enforcement activity near polling places.

## Noncitizen-voter challenges may affect eligible voters
The administration’s efforts to identify suspected noncitizen voters have raised concerns about eligible citizens being wrongly flagged or removed from voter rolls. The Justice Department sought to stop California from counting mail ballots from more than 20,000 voters it described as likely noncitizens. California disputed the list’s reliability. A Los Angeles County sample review found that some people on the list were not registered in the county or had never voted, while some registration forms indicated that the voters were born in the United States. Washington state officials also challenged a federal list of 400 names, saying it included citizens, people who were not registered and duplicate entries.

## Voting rules and election security present other risks
The report also examined state-level voting rules that could make it harder for ballots to be counted. In North Carolina, new mail-ballot procedures require voters to seal two envelopes, and the article cited reporting that ballot rejections increased when voters failed to complete the additional step. In Texas, reductions in polling locations could contribute to longer lines and confusion. Election officials were also concerned about cyberattacks, physical threats and false alarms at vote-counting sites. ProPublica recalled that false bomb threats disrupted counting locations in several states during the 2024 election, although those disruptions were brief.

## Legal challenges could continue after voting
Election lawyers were concerned that the administration’s pre-election actions could be followed by aggressive challenges after the results are known. The Justice Department sued Alaska seeking to block certification of the Senate result if Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan lost, arguing that another candidate with the same name could confuse voters. A federal judge dismissed that lawsuit. ProPublica noted that federal intervention in an active election is unusual and differs from the more typical process in which losing candidates challenge results in state court after voting ends. The election’s integrity will depend on officials administering voting and counting under the law and on political actors respecting the judicial process.
