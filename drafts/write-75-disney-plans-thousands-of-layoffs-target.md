Disney考虑裁减数千个岗位，部分管理层被要求提出20%成本节约目标

Disney正考虑新一轮成本削减。据Bloomberg报道，Walt Disney Co.要求部分管理人员提出年度成本节约目标，幅度达到20%，可能通过裁员和削减其他开支实现。Reuters随后转述Bloomberg报道，并指出公司尚未公布可能裁减的具体人数。报道反映的是内部讨论，并不代表Disney已经正式宣布裁员名单或完成重组。

## 成本目标可能覆盖多个部门

Bloomberg称，Disney部分业务可能受到更大影响，涉及主题乐园、电视网络和电影制作等部门。首席执行官Josh D’Amaro希望将部分节省下来的资金重新投入影视节目、技术、主题乐园和邮轮业务。公司希望在控制成本的同时，把资金转向管理层认为更具增长潜力的业务。

## 裁员规模尚未明确

目前公开报道没有给出最终裁员人数、部门名单或执行时间表。Reuters指出，Disney没有立即回应其置评请求。Bloomberg援引知情人士称，管理层讨论仍在进行，成本节约可能同时来自岗位削减和非人员费用控制。因此，将报道中的“数千个岗位”理解为已经确定的裁员数字并不准确。

## 公司此前已有多轮削减

Disney过去几年持续削减成本和员工人数。Reuters报道，公司在2023年削减约7000个岗位；此后在2025年又裁减数百个职位，并于2026年4月削减约1000个岗位。Reuters还在2026年9月报道，公司在人力资源和技术部门进行了数百人的裁员。公司截至2025财年末约有23.1万名员工。

## 娱乐产业面临结构性压力

Disney面临的压力并非单一因素。Reuters列出的挑战包括人工智能应用增加、电影票房表现疲弱、流媒体竞争加剧，以及国际游客前往主题乐园的增长放缓。传统电视收入下滑和观众转向社交媒体、在线视频，也推动媒体公司重新评估人员配置和内容投入。

## 节省成本与再投资之间的平衡

裁员可以在短期内降低薪酬和运营支出，但如果削减影响内容制作、技术研发或园区服务，也可能损害长期竞争力。D’Amaro据报道希望将部分节省资金重新投入核心业务，显示这轮讨论不仅是缩减规模，也涉及业务资源重新分配。真正影响Disney经营表现的，将是节约金额能否转化为更稳定的盈利和更有吸引力的内容与体验。

目前应把这则消息视为基于匿名知情人士的重组报道。Disney尚未公开确认裁员人数和时间安排，投资者仍需等待公司正式公告或后续财务披露。

Disney Considers Thousands of Job Cuts as Managers Face a 20% Cost-Savings Target

Walt Disney Co. is considering another major cost-reduction effort. Bloomberg reported that the company has asked some managers to identify annual savings of 20%, with reductions potentially coming from job eliminations as well as cuts to other expenses. Reuters subsequently reported the Bloomberg account and noted that the company had not specified how many positions could be affected. The report describes internal discussions and a possible restructuring direction; it is not an official announcement of a finalized layoff list or a completed restructuring.

## Cost targets could reach several business areas

Bloomberg reported that some parts of Disney's operations could be affected more heavily than others. The businesses mentioned include theme parks, television networks and the movie studio. Chief Executive Officer Josh D'Amaro reportedly intends to reinvest part of the savings in film and television programming, technology, theme parks and cruise businesses.

That approach suggests the company is considering more than a simple reduction in its workforce. Management appears to be looking at how to redirect resources among different parts of the business while pursuing lower operating costs. The scale and timing of any changes remain unclear, and the reported target does not mean every division will face the same percentage reduction.

## The number of layoffs has not been confirmed

The public reports do not provide a final number of employees who may lose their jobs, a list of affected departments or a timetable for implementation. Reuters said Disney did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Bloomberg cited people familiar with the discussions, who were not identified because the conversations were private.

The reported savings could come from eliminating positions and reducing non-labor expenses. It would therefore be inaccurate to treat the phrase "thousands of job cuts" as a confirmed final figure. The 20% figure refers to a savings target reportedly discussed with some managers, not necessarily a 20% reduction in every department's headcount.

## Disney has already gone through several rounds of cuts

Disney has pursued cost reductions for several years. Reuters reported that the company cut about 7,000 positions in 2023 as part of a broader cost-saving plan. It later eliminated several hundred positions in 2025 and about 1,000 jobs in April 2026. Reuters also reported several hundred recent cuts in human resources and technology in September 2026.

The company had approximately 231,000 employees at the end of fiscal 2025, according to Reuters. That workforce spans theme parks, television, film, streaming, technology and other operations. The reported new round would therefore take place after multiple rounds of restructuring across a large and diversified business.

## The entertainment industry faces structural pressure

Disney's challenges are not limited to one division. Reuters identified the growing use of artificial intelligence, weaker box-office revenue, intensifying streaming competition and slower international attendance at Disney theme parks as pressures facing the company. Traditional television businesses are also dealing with declining revenue as audiences shift toward social media, online video and streaming platforms.

These changes create difficult decisions about where to reduce spending and where to invest. Content production and technology require sustained funding, while theme parks and cruise businesses depend on customer demand and capital investment. A cost plan that is too broad could weaken the very operations management wants to strengthen.

## The test will be whether savings support growth

Layoffs can reduce payroll and operating expenses in the short term. But if cuts weaken content development, technology capabilities or customer service at parks, they could also damage long-term competitiveness. D'Amaro's reported intention to reinvest part of the savings in core businesses indicates that the discussions involve reallocating resources as well as reducing costs.

For investors and employees, the most important details remain unknown: the number of roles affected, which business units will bear the reductions, how quickly changes will be implemented and how much money will be redirected to growth initiatives. Until Disney confirms those details, the report should be treated as an account of internal planning based on unnamed sources rather than a completed corporate decision.
