# 佐治亚州州长选战最后民调几乎打平，Bottoms仅以0.03个百分点领先Jackson

## 最后一轮民调显示两人势均力敌
佐治亚州州长选举进入投票日之前，民主党候选人Keisha Lance Bottoms与共和党候选人Rick Jackson在InsiderAdvantage公布的最后一轮民调中几乎完全持平。WSB-TV报道，Bottoms支持率为49.03%，Jackson为49%，另有2%的受访选民尚未决定。两人原始数字相差0.03个百分点，而不是足以形成实际领先优势的差距。FOX 5 Atlanta也报道了这份调查，但其文字将差距写为0.3个百分点，与列出的49.03%和49.0%不完全一致。按公布的原始数字计算，差距应为0.03个百分点。

## 误差范围远大于候选人差距
这项调查访问了2500名可能投票的选民，公布的抽样误差范围为正负1.96个百分点。候选人之间0.03个百分点的差距远小于这一误差范围，因此不应将Bottoms描述为具有统计意义上的领先。WSB-TV和FOX 5 Atlanta都将结果形容为势均力敌。Georgia Public Broadcasting在选前报道中也称州长选战处于胶着状态。民调只是特定时间内的选民意向测量，并不能预测实际投票率、选民临时决定或最终计票结果。

## 选民将在两种不同路线之间作出选择
这场选举是民主党前亚特兰大市长Bottoms与医疗行业企业家Jackson之间的较量。美联社报道，Bottoms主张扩大Medicaid覆盖、提高教师和公共部门雇员薪酬，并对数据中心发展及执法部门使用人工智能监控技术施加限制。Jackson则提出逐步取消州个人所得税、提高公共雇员薪酬，并通过私人保险机构扩大医疗保障。美联社指出，Jackson以个人财富大量资助竞选，Bottoms则强调其公共服务经历和对州政府政策的不同方向。

## 两名候选人最后一天继续争取选民
Georgia Public Broadcasting和Atlanta News First报道，两名候选人在投票日前最后一天继续走访全州、争取选民支持。Bottoms在多个地区举行活动，Jackson则安排密集的州内行程。Atlanta Journal-Constitution将这场竞争描述为势均力敌的州级对决，并指出Jackson投入大量个人资金仍未在民调中形成明显优势。Reuters此前也把佐治亚州列为值得关注的州长选举之一，认为Bottoms若胜选将成为美国首位当选州长的黑人女性。

## 民调无法替代最终选票
这份最后民调的重要信息不是某位候选人取得微弱领先，而是双方支持率处于统计上无法区分的水平。抽样误差、选民参与率、未决定选民的最终选择以及各县投票构成，都可能影响最终结果。Axios此前对两人辩论的报道显示，竞选议题包括医疗保障、数据中心、监控技术和经济政策。随着选民在投票日作出决定，民调所呈现的胶着局面仍须由实际选票和官方计票来验证，不能把选前调查当成选举结果。

# Final Georgia Governor Poll Shows a Virtual Tie, with Bottoms Ahead by 0.03 Percentage Points

## The final survey finds the candidates virtually even
With Georgia’s gubernatorial election reaching Election Day, Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms and Republican Rick Jackson were virtually tied in the final poll released by InsiderAdvantage. WSB-TV reported that Bottoms received 49.03% support and Jackson received 49%, while 2% of likely voters remained undecided. The difference between the two published figures is 0.03 percentage points, not a margin large enough to suggest a meaningful lead. FOX 5 Atlanta also reported on the survey, but described the gap as 0.3 percentage points, which does not match the raw figures it published, 49.03% and 49.0%. Calculated from those figures, the difference is 0.03 percentage points.

## The margin of error is far larger than the gap
The survey included 2,500 likely voters and reported a margin of error of plus or minus 1.96 percentage points. The 0.03-point difference between the candidates is far smaller than that margin of error. It would therefore be misleading to describe Bottoms as holding a statistically meaningful advantage. Both WSB-TV and FOX 5 Atlanta characterized the contest as effectively tied. Georgia Public Broadcasting also described the governor’s race as deadlocked in its final-day campaign coverage. A poll measures voter preferences at a particular moment. It cannot determine who will turn out, how undecided voters will ultimately vote or what the final count will show.

## Voters face two different policy approaches
The contest pits Bottoms, a former mayor of Atlanta, against Jackson, a healthcare entrepreneur. The Associated Press reported that Bottoms supports expanding Medicaid eligibility, increasing pay for teachers and other public employees, and limiting data-center development and the expansion of artificial-intelligence surveillance technology used by law enforcement. Jackson has proposed phasing out Georgia’s personal income tax, raising public-employee pay and expanding healthcare coverage through partnerships with private insurers. The AP also reported that Jackson has used substantial personal wealth to finance his campaign, while Bottoms has emphasized her public-service record and a different approach to state policy.

## Both campaigns made a final push across Georgia
Georgia Public Broadcasting and Atlanta News First reported that both candidates spent the final day before the election traveling around the state and making last appeals to voters. Bottoms held events in several areas, while Jackson scheduled an intensive statewide itinerary. The Atlanta Journal-Constitution described the contest as a close and consequential matchup, noting that Jackson’s heavy personal spending had not produced a clear polling advantage. Reuters had previously listed Georgia among the gubernatorial races to watch, noting that a Bottoms victory would make her the first Black woman elected governor in the United States.

## The poll is not a substitute for the vote count
The most important takeaway from the final survey is not that one candidate has a narrow lead, but that the candidates’ support levels are statistically indistinguishable. Sampling error, turnout, the decisions of undecided voters and the geographic distribution of ballots could all affect the result. Earlier reporting by Axios on the candidates’ debate showed that healthcare, data centers, surveillance technology and economic policy were among the issues separating them. As voters cast ballots on Election Day, the close polling picture still has to be tested against actual votes and official counting. A pre-election survey should not be treated as an election result.
