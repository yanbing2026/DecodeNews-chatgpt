# 联邦法官驳回特朗普政府要求哈佛提交招生记录的诉讼

美国联邦地区法官 Myong Joun 11月2日驳回司法部针对 Harvard University 提起的诉讼。特朗普政府此前要求哈佛提交招生记录，以调查学校是否遵守禁止在招生中使用种族因素的相关规定。法官认为，政府没有充分说明在寻求法院强制执行信息要求前，已完成法律规定的必要程序。案件以不妨碍重新提起诉讼的方式驳回，法院没有裁定哈佛是否存在招生歧视。

## 法院认为政府程序依据不足

美联社报道，司法部指控哈佛阻碍调查，并要求法院命令学校交出招生资料。法官 Myong Joun 认为，政府未能充分证明在提起诉讼前满足了相关行政程序要求。裁决重点在于政府是否履行了要求对方自愿配合、明确告知后续执法行动等程序，而不是招生政策本身是否合法。

## 驳回不等于哈佛获得实体胜诉

法院以 without prejudice 方式驳回诉讼，意味着政府在补足程序或提出依据充分的新诉讼后，仍可能继续寻求相关资料。法院明确没有认定哈佛是否实施歧视，也没有裁定联邦机构是否可以依法要求特定范围的申请人级别数据。该裁决因此是对当前诉讼程序的判断，而非对调查实体问题的最终结论。

## 司法部调查与学校立场

特朗普政府在诉讼中称，哈佛没有配合调查，未能提供政府要求的招生记录。哈佛则主张，联邦机构没有遵守相关法规规定的程序，不能直接通过诉讼强制学校交出资料。The Washington Post 报道，政府在2月提起诉讼，称哈佛未提供用于核查其是否遵守最高法院招生裁决的记录。哈佛表示自己遵守民权法律并提供过数据。

## 行政程序是裁决关键

法官的备忘录指出，司法部没有充分说明其是否按照法规要求作出诉前决定，也没有充分交代通知哈佛如果不配合将采取何种具体行动。教育部的程序也需要独立评估，政府未能充分证明其完成了法规要求的额外劝说和通知步骤。法院认为，单纯经过一定时间，并不足以证明行政机关已经履行所有程序义务。

## 政府仍可补正后继续行动

法院指出，程序缺陷可能通过后续行政步骤或更充分的诉状得到补正，因此采用不妨碍重新起诉的方式驳回。裁决没有认定政府的资料要求在实体上无效，也没有禁止政府未来采取符合法规的调查行动。若司法部或教育部重新推进，仍需满足适用的行政程序和法律要求。

这项裁决将争议暂时限制在程序层面。哈佛是否遵守招生相关民权规定、政府能否合法取得申请人层级资料，以及未来是否会重新提起诉讼，都尚未由这次裁决决定。将案件驳回描述为法院认定哈佛没有歧视，或认定政府永远无权索取资料，均超出了裁决范围。

# Federal Judge Dismisses Trump Administration Lawsuit Seeking Harvard Admissions Records

A federal judge on November 2 dismissed a Trump administration lawsuit seeking to force Harvard University to provide admissions records to federal agencies. The Justice Department had sought the information as part of an investigation into whether the university was complying with restrictions on the use of race in admissions. U.S. District Judge Myong Joun concluded that the government had not adequately established that it completed the procedural steps required before seeking judicial enforcement. The case was dismissed without prejudice, and the court did not decide whether Harvard had discriminated.

## Court finds the government’s procedural showing insufficient

The Associated Press reported that the Justice Department accused Harvard of thwarting an investigation and asked the court to order the university to turn over admissions information. Joun found that the government had not sufficiently demonstrated that it satisfied the relevant administrative requirements before bringing the case.

The decision focused on the process for enforcing information demands, including whether the agencies had properly sought voluntary compliance and given the required notice of the action they would take if the university did not comply. The judge did not decide the underlying merits of Harvard’s admissions policies.

## Dismissal does not resolve the discrimination question

The case was dismissed without prejudice. That means the government may be able to pursue the matter again after taking additional administrative steps or filing a sufficiently supported complaint. The judge expressly did not determine whether Harvard had discriminated, whether federal agencies could lawfully seek appropriately defined applicant-level information, or whether the university could ultimately be required to provide such records.

The limited nature of the ruling is important. It is not a finding that Harvard’s admissions practices complied with every applicable law. Nor is it a permanent ruling that federal agencies can never request admissions data. It addresses whether the government had established the procedural basis for the judicial relief it sought in this particular case.

## The Justice Department’s investigation and Harvard’s position

The administration argued that Harvard had failed to provide records needed to assess whether the university was complying with federal requirements. The Washington Post reported that the government filed the lawsuit in February, saying Harvard had not provided records to ensure compliance with a Supreme Court ruling that prohibited affirmative action in college admissions.

Harvard argued that the agencies had not followed the required procedures in seeking the information. The university has said it complied with civil rights laws and provided data. Those positions remain the parties’ claims; the November 2 ruling did not make a final finding on the substantive dispute.

## Administrative steps were central to the decision

The court’s memorandum addressed the Justice Department and Education Department processes separately. It concluded that the government had not adequately shown that the Justice Department made the required pre-enforcement determination in the manner contemplated by the applicable regulation. The complaint also did not sufficiently allege that Harvard had been informed of the specific action the government would take if the university failed to comply.

The judge also found that the government had not adequately established that the Education Department completed the additional steps required before referring the matter for judicial enforcement. The passage of time between communications, by itself, was not enough to demonstrate that the regulatory process had been satisfied.

## The administration may still take further action

The court said the procedural deficiency could potentially be cured through additional administrative steps or a better-supported complaint. It therefore dismissed the case without prejudice. The ruling does not establish that the government’s underlying information demand is substantively invalid, and it does not prevent the agencies from proceeding after complying with the applicable requirements.

The next steps remain uncertain. The government may decide whether to take further administrative action, seek the information through a revised process or file another case. Any renewed effort would have to be assessed on its own record.

The immediate result is a procedural setback for the administration, not a final judgment on Harvard’s admissions practices. The questions of whether Harvard violated civil rights requirements, whether the agencies can obtain applicant-level records and whether a new lawsuit will be filed remain unresolved.
