# 欧洲多国超市冷藏系统遭网络攻击，比利时启动调查

欧洲多国超市的冰箱和冷冻设备11月2日出现异常升温，初步调查指向针对制冷控制系统的网络攻击。AFP 报道称，至少九个欧洲国家的零售商报告冷藏问题，部分食品因温度升高而被丢弃。比利时受影响较严重，联邦检察机关已启动调查。攻击者身份和动机目前仍未确认。

## 冷藏设备温度异常上升

Reuters 报道，攻击影响了比利时、卢森堡、瑞典和爱尔兰等地的超市设备，导致冷藏和冷冻系统温度升高。AFP 随后报道称，受影响国家至少包括比利时、爱沙尼亚、芬兰、爱尔兰、意大利、卢森堡、荷兰、葡萄牙和瑞典。不同媒体统计的国家和门店数量有所差异，可能与报道时间及新增通报有关。

## 比利时启动联邦调查

Euronews 报道，比利时联邦检察机关已就事件展开调查。当地媒体统计显示，多家零售连锁门店受到影响，包括 Delhaize、Carrefour、Intermarché 和 Spar。Carrefour 表示其约700家门店中约15家受到影响；IKEA 位于 Mons 的门店也报告冷冻设备异常。不同连锁企业披露的受影响门店数字并不完全相同。

## 多国零售商报告损失

AFP 报道，爱尔兰零售商 Musgrave 称有13家 SuperValu 门店受到影响；瑞典 Coop 表示旗下6家门店出现问题。荷兰至少17家企业的制冷系统也据报遭到攻击。温度异常可能迫使门店丢弃需要冷藏的食品，但各公司披露的损失金额并不一致。Reuters 报道，一家比利时肉店估计损失约15,000欧元。

## 与法国此前事件存在相似之处

此次事件发生在法国超市制冷系统遭遇类似网络攻击数周之后。AFP 报道称，法国此前的攻击影响数百家门店，造成食品损失。Euronews 援引制冷服务商说法称，比利时事件可能涉及与法国事件相似的制冷控制机制，但目前没有证据确认两起事件由同一组织实施。

## 攻击者身份和动机尚未确定

目前没有组织公开宣称对此次攻击负责，执法机构也尚未公布攻击者身份。比利时网络安全部门表示掌握相关事件，但没有提供更多技术细节。欧洲委员会官员警告，反复发生的制冷系统攻击可能威胁食品供应链和消费者信心。调查仍在进行，现阶段不应将事件归因于特定国家或组织。

# Cyberattacks Disrupt Supermarket Refrigeration Systems Across Europe as Belgium Opens Investigation

Supermarket refrigeration systems in several European countries were disrupted on November 2 in what authorities and retailers described as suspected cyberattacks. The incidents caused temperatures in refrigerators and freezers to rise, forcing some businesses to discard food. AFP reported that grocery stores in at least nine European countries experienced problems. Belgium was among the hardest-hit countries, and federal prosecutors opened an investigation. The attackers and their motives have not been identified.

## Refrigeration temperatures rise across multiple countries

Reuters reported that the incidents affected supermarket refrigeration systems in Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden and Ireland, among other locations. AFP later reported problems in at least nine countries: Belgium, Estonia, Finland, Ireland, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal and Sweden.

The lists of affected countries and stores varied across reports, in part because retailers and authorities were still identifying the extent of the disruption. The common feature was that refrigeration controls were affected and temperatures rose, creating a risk to perishable food.

## Belgium opens a federal investigation

Euronews reported that Belgium’s federal prosecutor’s office had opened an investigation. Several retail chains reported problems, including Delhaize, Carrefour, Intermarché and Spar. Carrefour said about 15 of its 700 stores in Belgium were affected. IKEA also reported that its Mons location experienced a refrigeration incident.

The number of affected stores differed across companies and reports. Some Belgian outlets cited dozens of affected locations across different chains, while retailers provided their own figures. Those counts should not be combined as if they came from a single final government tally.

## Retailers in other countries report disruption

AFP reported that Irish retailer Musgrave said 13 SuperValu stores were affected. Sweden’s Coop said six of its stores had experienced problems. In the Netherlands, refrigeration systems at at least 17 companies were reportedly attacked, according to a spokesperson for supplier Danfoss.

Rising temperatures can force retailers to discard food that can no longer be safely stored. Reuters reported that one Belgian butcher estimated losses of about 15,000 euros. The full financial impact across all affected businesses has not been established.

## Similarities to an earlier incident in France

The European disruption followed a similar cyberattack on supermarket refrigeration systems in France several weeks earlier. AFP reported that the earlier incident affected hundreds of stores and led to food losses. Euronews cited a refrigeration industry executive who said the Belgian incident appeared to involve a mechanism similar to the earlier French attack.

That similarity does not prove that the incidents were carried out by the same group. No organization has publicly claimed responsibility for the latest attacks, and investigators have not established whether the separate incidents are connected.

## The attackers and their motives remain unknown

Belgian authorities have opened an investigation, but officials have not publicly identified the attackers or explained their motives. The country’s cybersecurity authorities acknowledged the incident but did not provide detailed technical information. The European Commission has warned that repeated attacks on refrigeration systems could threaten food supply chains and consumer confidence.

The immediate operational issue is restoring safe refrigeration and determining which products must be discarded. The broader security question is how retailers protect connected systems that control essential equipment. Until investigators release more information, it would be premature to attribute the attacks to a particular country, organization or political motive.
