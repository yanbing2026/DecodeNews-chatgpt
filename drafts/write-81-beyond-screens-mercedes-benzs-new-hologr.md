Mercedes-Benz展示挡风玻璃全息显示原型：乘客可见内容与驾驶员隔离

Mercedes-Benz公布一项仍处于研发阶段的挡风玻璃全息显示技术，希望把汽车玻璃变成按需出现的信息界面。系统通过仪表台内的投影器，将图像投射到夹在挡风玻璃中的特殊透明薄膜上；薄膜使用全息光学元件（HOE）选择性反射投影光线，使图像只在特定观察角度可见。驾驶员看不到乘客侧显示内容，乘客也看不到驾驶员侧的内容。该技术并非科幻电影中的悬浮全息影像，而更接近一种扩展型抬头显示系统。

## 特殊薄膜控制图像可见角度

Mercedes-Benz介绍，投影器安装在仪表台内，挡风玻璃夹层中的透明薄膜包含全息光学元件，可以选择性反射投影器发出的特定波长光线。公司称，这种方式可让图像在阳光下仍保持可见，同时限制图像的可见角度。显示内容并非一直存在，系统只在需要时呈现导航、驾驶信息、兴趣点说明、视频通话或影音内容。

这项设计的重点之一，是让驾驶员和乘客拥有不同的信息视野。乘客侧显示可以用于娱乐或视频通话，而驾驶员侧可用于导航或驾驶相关提示。Mercedes-Benz认为，这种方式有机会减少传统乘客屏幕对内饰材料和设计的占用，同时避免驾驶员直接看到乘客正在观看的内容。

## 实车体验仍暴露原型缺点

Ars Technica和Edmunds记者在Mercedes-Benz研发设施体验了原型系统。Ars称，原型使用一辆右舵测试车，投影器安装位置和仪表台开孔仍较粗糙，运行时还可听到散热风扇声音。工程人员表示，量产设想中的投影器会更小，风扇噪声也会得到改善。

目前图像质量仍是明显限制。Ars指出，虽然原型显示效果比传统HUD更丰富，但与乘客触摸屏相比，分辨率、对比度和画面比例仍不理想；挡风玻璃倾斜角度也会使图像产生变形。Edmunds同样观察到图像不够锐利，投影内容会随挡风玻璃角度倾斜。Mercedes工程人员表示正在改进这些问题，因此不能把目前展示的效果当成最终产品表现。

## 不是量产功能或上市承诺

Mercedes-Benz官方说明，相关显示技术仍在研发阶段，目前不是面向消费者的功能，也没有宣布具体量产车型或上市时间。公司同时展示了嵌入车窗的Mini LED和Micro LED显示方案，以及面向电动车的Power Converter，但这些项目同样属于研发技术。WELT报道也将挡风玻璃显示描述为早期研发项目，而非已经进入销售车型的配置。

## 目标是减少屏幕而非制造新屏幕

Mercedes-Benz近年来在部分车型中使用大型乘客屏幕和横贯式显示屏，但公司称部分客户仍偏好木饰板、金属和实体控制件。Ars引用公司工程人员的说法指出，品牌正在寻找在提供数字功能的同时保留传统豪华内饰质感的方法。全息显示概念因此并非单纯增加一块屏幕，而是尝试把信息显示隐藏在玻璃结构中，需要时才出现。

## 何时进入量产仍未确定

目前尚无证据表明这套系统会在近期车型中提供。即使光学原理已经展示，实际量产仍需解决清晰度、画面比例、安装空间、成本、耐久性和驾驶安全等问题。Totalcar的现场报道认为，原型展示具有视觉吸引力，但乘客是否真正需要这种显示方式仍有疑问。对消费者而言，当前更准确的理解是：Mercedes-Benz正在测试一种新型挡风玻璃显示方案，距离量产和普及仍有距离。

Mercedes-Benz Shows Holographic Windshield Display Prototype That Separates Passenger and Driver Views

Mercedes-Benz has revealed a windshield holographic display technology that remains under development and is intended to turn vehicle glass into an information surface that appears only when needed. A projector inside the dashboard sends images toward a special transparent film laminated into the windshield. The film uses holographic optical elements, or HOE, to selectively reflect the projector's light so that content is visible only from particular viewing angles. The driver cannot see what is displayed on the passenger side, and the passenger cannot see content intended for the driver. The system is not a floating hologram like those in science-fiction films. It is closer to an advanced form of head-up display.

## A special film controls viewing angles

Mercedes-Benz says the projector is housed in the instrument panel and sends images to a transparent film integrated into the windshield. The film contains holographic optical elements that selectively reflect specific wavelengths of light from the projector. The company says this arrangement can keep the image visible even in sunlight while restricting the viewing angle. The display is not permanently present. It can show navigation information, driving data, context about points of interest, video calls or entertainment when required.

One of the concept's central features is that the driver and passenger can have separate information views. The passenger-side display could be used for entertainment or video calls, while the driver's side could show navigation or driving-related information. Mercedes-Benz sees the approach as a way to offer digital features without occupying as much dashboard space with conventional passenger screens or requiring the cabin design to revolve around large display panels.

## Prototype demonstrations reveal remaining problems

Journalists from Ars Technica and Edmunds experienced the prototype at Mercedes-Benz development facilities. Ars reported that the test system was installed in a right-hand-drive vehicle and that the prototype projector sat in a relatively crude opening in the dashboard. A cooling fan was also audible during operation. Mercedes engineers said a production-intent projector could be much smaller and that the fan noise would not be present in a future production version.

Image quality remains a significant limitation. Ars said the prototype offered more information than a conventional HUD but still fell short of a passenger touchscreen in resolution, contrast and image proportions. The angle of the windshield also distorted the projected picture. Edmunds similarly found that the image was not particularly sharp and that the projected content followed the slope of the windshield. Mercedes engineers said they were working on improvements, so the demonstration should not be mistaken for the performance of a finished product.

## Mercedes has not announced a production application

Mercedes-Benz says the display technologies remain under development. They are not customer-facing features, and the company has not announced a production model or launch date for the holographic windshield system. The company also presented Mini LED and Micro LED technologies integrated into vehicle windows, as well as a Power Converter concept for electric vehicles. Those projects are also development technologies rather than confirmed production equipment. WELT likewise described the windshield display as an early research project, not a feature already available in vehicles for sale.

## The aim is to reduce visible screens, not add another one

Mercedes-Benz has introduced large passenger displays and wide dashboard screens in some recent vehicles, but the company says some customers still prefer traditional materials such as wood, metal and physical controls. Ars quoted Mercedes engineers describing the challenge of providing the technology customers expect while retaining the feel of a luxury interior. The holographic display is therefore not simply an attempt to add another screen. It is an effort to place an information surface inside the glass structure and make it appear only when it is needed.

## A production timeline remains unknown

There is currently no evidence that the system will be offered in an upcoming production vehicle. Even though the optical concept has been demonstrated, a commercial version would still need to address image sharpness, aspect ratio, packaging, cost, durability and driver-safety requirements. Totalcar's hands-on report described the prototype as visually impressive but questioned whether passengers would have a meaningful need for the feature. For consumers, the most accurate description is that Mercedes-Benz is testing a new windshield display approach, but it remains some distance from production and widespread use.
