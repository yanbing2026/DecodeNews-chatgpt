# 《Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered》保留原作乐趣，但解锁内容需要大量重复游玩

GameSpot 在11月2日发布《Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered》评测，认为这款重制版保留了2002年原作的核心玩法和怪兽对战乐趣，但单人模式中的解锁过程需要长时间重复游玩，削弱了游戏原本轻松、快速的街机体验。评测认为，与朋友一起进行本地多人对战时，游戏最能体现其魅力；但对希望快速体验全部怪兽和模式的玩家而言，重复刷取会成为明显负担。

## 怪兽破坏场景仍然有趣

GameSpot 评测作者 Kurt Indovina 表示，游戏最吸引人的地方是操控怪兽在城市中战斗、摧毁建筑并把对手击飞。重制版在机制上基本忠于2002年原作，玩家可以选择不同怪兽，使用近战动作、投掷建筑物和特殊攻击。评测认为，这种夸张的怪兽混战本身就具有娱乐性，尤其适合多人一起游玩。

## 初始可用角色数量有限

评测称，游戏开始时可直接使用的怪兽为12种中的4种，分别是 Godzilla 1990、Gigan、Megalon 和 Anguirus。不同怪兽在动作和能力上存在差异，例如 Gigan 可以传送，Megalon 可以钻入地下攻击对手。飞行类怪兽如 King Ghidorah 和 Rodan 不在初始开放名单中，需要通过游戏进程解锁。

## 解锁过程拖慢游戏节奏

GameSpot 对重制版最明显的批评，是解锁怪兽、关卡和模式需要长时间重复游玩。评测认为，原作强调快速上手和短局对战，但重制版把大量解锁目标放进单人流程，导致玩家为了使用特定怪兽而进行重复挑战。对于只想和朋友快速开战的玩家，这种设计与游戏的街机式定位并不完全匹配。

## 多人对战更能发挥优势

评测特别肯定本地多人玩法。游戏支持最多4名玩家同场混战，在 Nintendo Switch 2 上还可以通过拆分 Joy-Con 让朋友快速加入。多个怪兽同时在场地中发射光束、投掷建筑和互相追逐，能产生混乱而有趣的场面。评测认为，和朋友一起游玩时，游戏的乐趣明显高于单人反复解锁内容的过程。

## 重制保留原作，也保留了局限

GameSpot 认为，重制版忠实还原了原作的动作和整体结构，但也保留了原作玩法深度有限的特点。部分怪兽动作相似，单人模式容易显得重复。评测没有把它描述为全面失败，而是认为它适合怀念原作、喜欢怪兽对战或经常进行本地多人游戏的玩家；但若期待现代格斗游戏般复杂的系统，可能会感到内容不足。

这款重制版的主要取舍在于忠实与现代化之间：它让经典怪兽对战重新出现在现代平台，却没有完全解决原作结构和内容解锁节奏的问题。

# Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered Keeps the Fun but Makes Unlocking Content a Grind

GameSpot published its review of Godzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee Remastered on November 2, describing a faithful revival of the 2002 monster brawler that preserves much of the original’s chaotic appeal. The review praised the spectacle of giant monsters fighting through destructible environments, particularly in local multiplayer. Its main criticism was the amount of repetitive play required to unlock monsters, stages and modes, which can undermine the game’s quick, arcade-style design.

## Monster battles remain the main attraction

Reviewer Kurt Indovina said the game is at its best when players are simply controlling giant creatures and causing destruction. The remaster largely follows the mechanics of the original release, allowing monsters to fight with close-range attacks, special moves and environmental objects. Players can throw buildings, launch opponents across the arena and use distinctive attacks to create the kind of exaggerated spectacle associated with kaiju battles.

The review found that this central idea still works. Watching monsters collide in a city and send one another through buildings remains entertaining, especially when several players are involved. The appeal is less about a deep competitive fighting system and more about the spectacle and unpredictability of the encounters.

## Only four monsters are available at the start

GameSpot said players begin with access to four of the game’s 12 monsters: Godzilla 1990, Gigan, Megalon and Anguirus. The roster includes different creature types and abilities. Gigan can teleport, while Megalon can burrow underground and attack opponents when it emerges. Flying monsters such as King Ghidorah and Rodan are among the characters that players need to unlock.

The roster provides variety, but the review noted that many monsters share similar basic moves. Individual quirks and special abilities help distinguish them, yet the early experience can make some fights feel alike.

## Unlocking content slows the pace

The review’s most significant complaint concerns the time required to unlock additional monsters, stages and modes. GameSpot said the process involves hours of repeated play. That structure works against the game’s pick-up-and-play identity, particularly for players who want to choose a favorite monster and start a quick match.

The issue is especially noticeable because the original concept is built around short, accessible battles. Requiring extended single-player grinding before players can access the full roster and selection of modes makes the remaster feel more laborious than its arcade-style design suggests.

## Local multiplayer brings out the game’s strengths

GameSpot was more positive about local multiplayer. The game supports matches with as many as four players, and the review highlighted the convenience of playing on Nintendo Switch 2, where a Joy-Con can be detached and handed to another person. With several monsters moving around the arena, firing beams and throwing pieces of the environment, matches can become chaotic in a way that suits the series.

The reviewer found that playing with friends makes the experience more enjoyable than progressing through the single-player unlock structure. The social element helps turn the simple mechanics into a source of spontaneous fun.

## A faithful remaster that retains old limitations

The review’s overall assessment was that the remaster preserves the original game’s identity, including both its strengths and its limitations. The straightforward combat and spectacle remain appealing, but the fighting mechanics are not especially deep, and the unlocking process can make the single-player experience repetitive.

That does not make the remaster a failure. It may appeal to players who remember the original, enjoy Godzilla and other kaiju, or regularly play local multiplayer games. But players expecting a modern competitive fighter with extensive mechanical depth may find the experience limited.

The central trade-off is fidelity. The remaster brings a recognizable monster brawler to modern platforms, but it does not fully modernize the structure of its progression. Its best moments come from playing with others, while its weakest moments come from the work required to unlock the content that makes those matches varied.
