# 报道称特朗普政府拟收窄孕期职场保障，生育治疗与堕胎安排或受影响

## 政府考虑修改现行规则
The New York Times报道，特朗普政府正考虑缩小与怀孕和生殖健康相关的职场合理便利范围。七名了解内部讨论的人士向该报透露，平等就业机会委员会（EEOC）可能修改《孕妇公平法》（Pregnant Workers Fairness Act）的实施规则。该法于2022年通过，原本旨在让怀孕员工能够继续工作，并获得饮水、如厕、避免重体力劳动或参加产检等合理安排。

## 争议集中在法律覆盖范围
争论焦点是EEOC现行规则对“怀孕、分娩或相关医疗状况”的解释是否过宽。支持修改的人认为，规则将保护延伸至堕胎、生育治疗及其他并非员工正在怀孕时发生的生殖健康情况，超出了国会立法原意。支持现行规则的人则认为，怀孕及其相关医疗影响并不局限于孕期本身，法律应覆盖更广泛的医疗需求，避免员工因生殖健康问题失去工作机会。

## 可能影响生育治疗与堕胎相关便利
报道指出，政府正在考虑取消对某些非孕期生殖健康状况的明确保护，并研究扩大雇主宗教豁免的可能性。若规则收窄，接受生育治疗的员工可能失去目前可申请的部分工作时间调整或休假安排；堕胎相关便利也可能受到影响。报道强调，讨论中的方案尚未最终确定，不能将可能的修改写成已经生效的政策。

## 现行法律仍提供合理便利
《孕妇公平法》要求受该法约束的雇主，为因怀孕、分娩或相关医疗状况而受到限制的合格员工提供合理便利，除非造成不当困难。现行规则列出的便利包括额外休息、饮水、调整工作时间、暂时减少体力工作以及产检假等。争议并非是否保留最基本的孕期保障，而是法律中“相关医疗状况”的边界，以及雇主在宗教信仰等理由下可以主张何种豁免。

## 后续取决于正式提案与程序
The New York Times称，EEOC可能在本月提出规则修改方案。正式提案发布后，通常还需经过公众意见征询等行政程序，最终规则才可能改变现有权利义务。在正式程序完成之前，雇主和员工仍应依据现行法律及有效规则处理合理便利申请。未来值得关注的是提案如何界定生育治疗、堕胎和其他生殖健康状况，以及委员会如何回应公众意见。

# Trump Administration Reportedly Weighs Narrowing Pregnancy-Related Workplace Protections

## Officials are considering changes to existing rules
The New York Times reported that the Trump administration is preparing to consider narrowing workplace accommodations related to pregnancy and reproductive health. Seven people familiar with the discussions told the newspaper that the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission may revise regulations implementing the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act. Congress passed the law in 2022 to help pregnant employees remain at work by requiring reasonable accommodations such as bathroom and water breaks, relief from heavy lifting and time away for prenatal appointments.

## The dispute centers on the law’s scope
The debate concerns how broadly the EEOC’s current rules interpret the phrase “pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions.” Supporters of a narrower approach argue that the regulations extended protections to abortion, fertility treatment and other reproductive-health conditions that do not involve an employee being pregnant, going beyond what Congress intended. Supporters of the current rule say pregnancy-related medical needs do not end with an ongoing pregnancy and that broad coverage helps prevent workers from losing employment because of reproductive-health conditions.

## Fertility treatment and abortion-related accommodations could be affected
The report said the administration is considering removing explicit protections for some conditions affecting people who are not pregnant and examining broader religious exemptions for employers. If the rules are narrowed, employees undergoing fertility treatment could lose some clearly stated accommodation protections, including scheduling changes or time off for treatment. Accommodations related to abortion could also be affected. The newspaper stressed that the plans were still under discussion and had not been finalized.

## The current law requires reasonable accommodations
The Pregnant Workers Fairness Act requires covered employers to provide reasonable accommodations to qualified employees whose limitations relate to pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions, unless doing so would create an undue hardship. Existing rules describe accommodations that can include additional breaks, access to water, schedule adjustments, temporary relief from physically demanding duties and time off for prenatal care. The dispute is not simply whether basic pregnancy protections will remain. It concerns the boundaries of “related medical conditions” and the circumstances in which employers may claim exemptions, including on religious grounds.

## Any change must go through a formal process
The New York Times reported that the EEOC could propose revisions as soon as this month. A proposal would not itself change the current rule; the agency would need to proceed through the applicable administrative process, including an opportunity for public comment, before a final rule could take effect. Until then, employers and workers remain subject to the existing law and regulations. Key questions will include how the proposal defines fertility treatment, abortion and other reproductive-health conditions, and how the commission responds to public comments.
