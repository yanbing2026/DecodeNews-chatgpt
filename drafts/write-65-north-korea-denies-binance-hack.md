# 朝鲜否认参与币安巨额加密货币失窃案，FBI归因与特朗普表态出现落差

## 金正恩胞妹公开驳斥美方指控
朝鲜方面否认参与全球最大加密货币交易平台Binance遭遇的巨额失窃事件。朝鲜劳动党高级官员、领导人金正恩的妹妹Kim Yo Jong通过朝中社发表声明，将美国联邦调查局（FBI）把袭击归因于朝鲜黑客的说法称为“荒谬言论”和“严重政治挑衅”。她指责华盛顿试图把朝鲜塑造成“网络犯罪国家”，并称美国一再把国际网络犯罪事件与朝鲜联系起来。她还表示，朝鲜将重新考虑与美国关系中的一些问题，但没有说明具体措施。

## FBI称攻击来自朝鲜黑客组织
FBI在此前发布的网络安全通报中表示，朝鲜对Binance发生的虚拟资产盗窃负责，并将这次行动归入其所称的TraderTraitor网络活动。FBI称，袭击发生在2026年10月29日前后，被盗资产主要被转换为以太币，执法部门预计攻击者还会通过去中心化金融和即时兑换服务继续转移资金。FBI呼吁虚拟资产服务商和区块链相关企业拦截与涉案地址有关的交易。FBI的通报是美国执法机构的正式归因，但朝鲜否认责任，公开报道目前没有提供能够独立确认幕后操作者身份的完整证据链。

## 失窃金额与责任归属需要区分
Binance及媒体对失窃金额的表述略有不同。Binance相关报道将损失估算为约63亿美元，而FBI通报使用的是超过60亿美元。美联社指出，这起事件可能成为有记录以来规模最大的加密货币交易平台失窃案，并提到朝鲜黑客此前也被指与Bybit遭遇的重大攻击有关。The Block和CoinDesk的报道则介绍了区块链分析机构如何追踪资金流向，以及调查人员为何怀疑攻击与朝鲜相关组织有关。这些分析构成归因背景，但链上资金移动本身并不能单独证明具体行为人的国家身份。

## 特朗普公开表示调查仍在了解中
美联社报道，美国总统Donald Trump在被问及此案时表示，调查人员仍在了解情况，并称“我们不知道是不是朝鲜”。这一表态与FBI通报中明确归因朝鲜的措辞形成落差。两者并非同一性质的文件：FBI发布的是执法机构调查结论，特朗普的回答则是对记者提问的公开回应。现阶段不宜把总统的简短回答理解为撤回FBI结论，也不能忽略它反映出的政府公开表态并不完全一致。

## 交易平台恢复服务但调查仍在继续
围绕失窃事件的另一条线索，是Binance逐步恢复部分资产和区块链网络的提款功能。Reuters报道，Binance联席首席执行官Richard Teng称，用户提款在最初激增后已趋于正常，平台也补回了大部分受影响代币。DW和其他加密货币媒体同样报道了提款恢复进展。不过，平台恢复服务并不等于资金已经全部追回，也不代表攻击者身份争议已经解决。朝鲜否认、美方归因、链上调查和交易平台善后仍是几条需要分别核实的线索。

## 朝鲜否认并未终结归因争议
朝鲜长期否认参与多起国际网络攻击，而美国及网络安全研究机构过去曾多次把大型加密货币盗窃归因于朝鲜相关组织。美联社援引专家观点指出，非法网络活动被认为是朝鲜获取外汇、维持经济活动和支持武器项目的来源之一；但这类背景不能替代对本案证据的检验。后续关键问题包括执法机构能否公布更多可核实的技术证据、被盗资产能否追回，以及国际平台和服务商是否会进一步冻结相关地址。

# North Korea Denies Role in Binance Heist as FBI Attribution Faces Questions

## Kim Yo Jong rejects the U.S. accusation
North Korea has denied involvement in the massive theft from Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange. Kim Yo Jong, the influential sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un and a senior ruling-party official, dismissed the U.S. allegation as “absurd rhetoric” and a “grave political provocation” in a statement carried by North Korean state media. She accused Washington of trying to brand North Korea a “cyber criminal state” and of repeatedly linking the country to international cybercrime. Kim also said Pyongyang had issues to reconsider in its dealings with the United States, but she did not specify what steps North Korea might take.

## The FBI attributes the attack to a North Korean group
The Federal Bureau of Investigation said in a cybersecurity notice that North Korea was responsible for the theft of more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance, on or about October 29, 2026. The FBI refers to the activity as TraderTraitor. It said investigators had observed the actors converting stolen assets primarily into Ether and expected further laundering through decentralized finance platforms and instant-swap services. The agency urged virtual-asset service providers, blockchain infrastructure operators and other companies to block transactions connected to addresses used by the suspected actors. The notice represents a formal attribution by a U.S. law-enforcement agency. North Korea rejects the accusation, however, and public reporting has not provided a complete body of independently verifiable evidence that establishes the operators’ national identity.

## The reported loss and the attribution are separate questions
Coverage has used slightly different figures for the amount stolen. Binance-related reporting has described the loss as approximately $6.3 billion, while the FBI notice says more than $6 billion. The Associated Press described the incident as potentially the largest cryptocurrency-exchange theft on record and noted that North Korean hackers had also been blamed for a major attack on Bybit. Reporting by The Block and CoinDesk has examined how blockchain analytics firms traced the stolen funds and why investigators suspect links to North Korean actors. Those technical analyses form part of the attribution case, but the movement of cryptocurrency across wallets does not, by itself, establish the nationality of the people controlling them.

## Trump says investigators are still learning about the case
The Associated Press reported that President Donald Trump, when asked about the incident, said investigators were still learning about it and added, “We don’t know if it’s North Korea.” The comment contrasts with the FBI’s direct language attributing the theft to North Korea. The two statements are not identical in nature: the FBI published an agency notice describing its investigative conclusion, while Trump was responding briefly to reporters’ questions. His remark should not automatically be read as a formal reversal of the FBI’s assessment. It does, however, show that public statements from the U.S. government have not been entirely consistent in how they describe the level of certainty.

## Binance is restoring services while the investigation continues
A separate development concerns Binance’s efforts to restore withdrawals across affected assets and blockchain networks. Reuters reported that co-CEO Richard Teng said withdrawals had normalized after an initial surge and that most affected tokens had been replenished. Deutsche Welle and cryptocurrency-focused outlets also reported on the phased restoration of withdrawals. The reopening of services does not mean all stolen funds have been recovered, nor does it settle the dispute over who carried out the attack. The exchange’s recovery effort, the FBI’s attribution, North Korea’s denial and the tracing of funds remain related but distinct parts of the story.

## The denial does not resolve the attribution dispute
North Korea has routinely rejected allegations linking it to international cyberattacks, while U.S. authorities and cybersecurity researchers have attributed several major cryptocurrency thefts to North Korean-linked groups. The Associated Press cited outside experts who say illicit cyber activity is believed to provide North Korea with foreign currency and help support its economy and weapons programs. That broader context is relevant, but it cannot substitute for evidence specific to this incident. The next important questions are whether investigators release more verifiable technical details, whether additional stolen assets can be recovered or frozen, and whether cryptocurrency platforms and service providers take further action against the wallets identified in the investigation.
