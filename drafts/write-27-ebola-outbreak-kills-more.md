# 刚果（金）埃博拉疫情死亡人数超过5300，传播已波及多个国家

非洲疾病控制与预防中心（Africa CDC）公布的最新数据称，非洲本轮埃博拉疫情累计死亡人数已达5,376人，其中5,372人位于刚果民主共和国，肯尼亚和乌干达各报告2人死亡。疫情由较少见的 Bundibugyo 病毒株引起，刚果（金）仍是疫情中心。随着病例跨境出现，多个国家正在加强监测和应对。

## 最新死亡数据出现大幅上升

Al Jazeera 11月3日援引 Africa CDC 周一数据报道，疫情累计死亡5,376人，累计感染人数约10,444人，病例分布在刚果（金）、乌干达和肯尼亚。该数字与此前媒体报道的刚果（金）单国死亡数据并非完全相同，统计时应区分国家与区域口径。Africanews 转述刚果（金）国家生物医学研究所数据指出，死亡数字上升部分来自对伊图里省数据的追溯调整，并非全部发生在短时间内的新死亡。

## 疫情从刚果（金）跨境传播

刚果（金）在5月中旬宣布本国第17次埃博拉疫情，也是该国迄今规模最大的一次。乌干达此前报告20例病例，绝大多数与来自刚果（金）的旅行者有关，并在8月宣布疫情结束。肯尼亚10月报告9例感染和2例死亡，这是该国首次记录到埃博拉病例。尼日利亚曾对东北部两名疑似病例进行两轮检测，结果均为阴性；该国此前报告的一名输入病例已转往瑞士接受治疗。

## 病毒株缺少获批疫苗和疗法

本轮疫情由 Bundibugyo 病毒株引起。Al Jazeera 报道称，目前没有获批的疫苗或特效治疗方案可以针对这一病毒株。近期开展的 Ervebo 疫苗临床试验针对的是更常见的 Zaire 病毒株；不能将其有效性直接等同于对 Bundibugyo 病毒株有效。疫情应对因此仍高度依赖病例发现、隔离、感染控制、医疗照护和社区合作。

## 冲突和社区阻力增加防控难度

BBC 10月31日报道，刚果（金）已有26个省中的8个受到疫情影响，伊图里省仍是疫情中心。当地冲突使卫生人员更难进入部分地区。BBC 还报道，曾有卫生人员在响应疫情警报时遭袭，另有地方政治人物因宣传防疫措施而遇害。此类事件会削弱社区信任、妨碍接触追踪，并增加医护人员开展工作的风险。

## 国际监测仍需保持谨慎

疫情病例和死亡数字来自不同机构、不同统计日期，且部分数据经历追溯修订。Africa CDC 的区域统计与刚果（金）卫生部门的单国报告不应简单相加。当前可以确认的是，刚果（金）承担绝大多数病例和死亡，肯尼亚及乌干达也出现确诊病例，而尼日利亚疑似病例检测结果为阴性。疫情是否继续扩散，取决于监测、医疗资源、边境协作以及受影响社区能否获得可信信息和支持。

# Ebola Outbreak Death Toll Tops 5,300, With Most Deaths in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The death toll from the latest Ebola outbreak in Africa has exceeded 5,300, according to figures reported by the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention. Data cited by Al Jazeera on November 3 put the regional total at 5,376 deaths: 5,372 in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, with two each in Kenya and Uganda. The outbreak is centered in the DRC and is caused by the Bundibugyo strain, a less common form of the virus for which no approved vaccine or specific treatment is currently available.

## Latest figures show a severe toll

Al Jazeera, citing Africa CDC data released on Monday, reported approximately 10,444 infections across the DRC, Uganda and Kenya, alongside the 5,376 deaths. The figures should be read with attention to their geographic and reporting dates. A regional total is not interchangeable with a DRC-only health ministry count, and different agencies may publish figures based on different reporting cutoffs.

Africanews reported that the DRC’s National Institute of Biomedical Research had recorded 5,372 deaths in the country. It also noted that a substantial increase in the reported death count followed a retrospective adjustment involving local data processing and the Ituri Province outbreak management team. That explanation matters: the increase in the published total should not be described as if all the additional deaths occurred in a single day.

## The outbreak has crossed borders

The DRC declared its 17th Ebola outbreak in mid-May. It has become the country’s largest recorded outbreak, according to the reporting cited by Al Jazeera and the BBC. Uganda previously recorded 20 cases, most involving people who had traveled from the DRC, and declared itself Ebola-free in August.

Kenya reported nine infections and two deaths in October, marking the country’s first recorded Ebola cases. Nigeria’s health authorities said two suspected cases in the northeast had tested negative after two rounds of testing. The country had earlier reported an imported case in October, which was transferred to Switzerland for treatment. A separate case involving a health worker returning from the DRC was reported in France in July, according to Al Jazeera.

These developments show why regional surveillance is important. The presence of cases in neighboring countries does not mean that transmission is occurring at the same scale everywhere. The DRC remains the center of the outbreak, while other countries have reported smaller numbers and have taken measures to identify and isolate suspected infections.

## The Bundibugyo strain presents a treatment challenge

The current outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola. Al Jazeera reported that there are no approved vaccines or treatments specifically for this strain. Clinical trials have recently begun involving the Ervebo vaccine, which has demonstrated effectiveness against the more common Zaire strain. That does not establish that the vaccine is effective against Bundibugyo, and the two strains should not be treated as interchangeable.

In the absence of an approved strain-specific vaccine or treatment, outbreak control depends on identifying cases, isolating infected patients, protecting health workers, tracing contacts and communicating clearly with affected communities. Medical teams also provide supportive care, but the available reporting does not identify a proven, approved Bundibugyo-specific therapy.

## Conflict and mistrust complicate containment

The BBC reported on October 31 that eight of the DRC’s 26 provinces had been affected and that Ituri Province remained the epidemic’s center. The region’s insecurity makes it harder for health workers to reach some communities and maintain surveillance.

The BBC also described attacks on people involved in the response. A nurse was killed and another health worker injured while responding to an Ebola alert, after local residents accused them of contaminating water and spreading the disease. The report also described a local politician who was beaten to death after promoting measures intended to slow transmission. Such incidents can undermine trust, interrupt contact tracing and expose health workers to additional danger.

## Regional figures require careful interpretation

The reported totals come from different authorities and reporting periods. Africa CDC’s regional count should not be added to the DRC’s national total, because the national figures are already included in the regional figure. Some published totals have also been revised retrospectively. The safest reading is that the DRC accounts for the overwhelming majority of the reported deaths, while Kenya and Uganda have also recorded deaths.

The outbreak’s next phase will depend on whether authorities can maintain surveillance, reach affected areas, protect medical teams and build cooperation with local communities. The latest numbers demonstrate the scale of the emergency, but they do not by themselves explain how quickly transmission is changing in each location.
