# 美国2026年中期选举进入最后冲刺，特朗普为共和党助选、民主党期待翻盘

美国2026年中期选举投票日前夕，两党候选人继续在关键州争取选民。总统 Donald Trump 加大对共和党候选人的支持，并要求支持者把这次选举视作对其执政表现的投票；民主党则公开表达夺回国会控制权的希望。选举结果将决定国会两院未来的政治格局，也将检验选民对特朗普政府经济、外交和治理表现的评价。

## 两党在最后时刻争取选票

The New York Times 11月2日的现场报道显示，候选人正在竞选活动的最后数小时内密集拉票。特朗普持续为共和党候选人站台，并敦促支持者积极投票。民主党方面则试图把选举塑造成对总统执政的公投，强调生活成本、对外政策和政府运作等议题。虽然民主党对赢得众议院抱有较高期待，但参议院的争夺仍存在较大不确定性。

## 国会控制权是核心悬念

这次选举将改写众议院和参议院的席位分布。The New York Times 报道指出，民主党人希望同时赢回两院，但不同州的选区结构、候选人表现和选民投票率都会影响最终结果。参议院战场尤其复杂，德州、阿拉斯加和缅因州等地的竞选受到全国关注。即使某一方在全国性民调中占优，也不能直接推导出每个州的结果。

## 选民对总统的评价影响投票

中期选举通常被视为对现任总统执政表现的一次阶段性检验。报道指出，特朗普试图让共和党候选人与他的政治议程保持一致，同时也强调自己仍是动员共和党选民的核心人物。民主党则希望将选民对物价、伊朗冲突和政府施政方式的不满转化为投票支持。候选人的地方议题与全国政治情绪将在选举日交织。

## 选举程序和信任问题受到关注

The New York Times 的现场报道还提到，围绕选举管理和选举公正性的争议持续发酵。特朗普此前多次质疑选举程序，联邦政府近期的行动也引发部分观察人士对选举干预和选民受到威吓的担忧。选举监督组织与志愿者计划在投票地点观察情况。投票过程中的争议可能影响选后对结果的接受程度，但选举结果仍应以各州依法统计和认证的结果为准。

## 最后一轮竞选不等于结果已定

投票日前的竞选气氛和民调不能代替正式计票。早期投票数据可以显示参与度和选民结构，却不能直接揭示选民投给了谁。The New York Times 同日的另一篇分析称，已有超过5,000万名美国人提前投票，但各州规则和数据披露方式不同，早期投票数据的解释存在限制。候选人和政党都在根据这些信号作出判断，最终席位仍需等待投票和计票。

这次中期选举不仅决定国会席位，也将影响特朗普政府剩余任期的立法空间、预算谈判和监督压力。若民主党取得一院或两院控制权，国会调查与行政部门对抗可能增加；若共和党守住多数，特朗普政府推动议程的空间则可能保持。上述政治影响取决于正式选举结果，投票日前的预测并非最终结论。

# 2026 Midterms Enter Their Final Hours as Trump Campaigns for Republicans and Democrats Hope to Flip Congress

The 2026 U.S. midterm elections entered their final campaign hours on November 2, with candidates making last-minute appeals in closely watched races across the country. President Donald Trump continued campaigning for Republicans and urged supporters to treat the election as a test of his presidency. Democrats, meanwhile, expressed confidence that they could regain control of Congress. The outcome will shape the balance of power in Washington and provide a nationwide measure of voter sentiment toward the administration.

## Both parties make their final appeals

The New York Times’ live coverage on November 2 described candidates sprinting through the final hours before Election Day. Trump continued to make the case for Republican candidates and encouraged his supporters to turn out. Democrats sought to frame the contests as a referendum on the president’s record, pointing to concerns involving household costs, foreign policy and the administration’s approach to governing.

The final campaign push does not mean that either party has secured a result. Candidates still face state-by-state contests in which local issues, turnout and the quality of individual campaigns can matter as much as national political sentiment. The last hours are particularly important for campaigns trying to reach voters who have not yet made a decision or who have not yet cast a ballot.

## Control of Congress remains the central question

The elections will determine the composition of the House and Senate. Democrats are hoping to regain control of both chambers, but the Senate map presents a more complicated challenge. Races in states including Texas, Alaska and Maine have drawn national attention. The outcome in each state will depend on its own candidates, electorate and voting patterns rather than on a single national measure of political support.

A change in control of either chamber would affect the relationship between Congress and the White House. A Democratic majority could increase congressional scrutiny of the administration and complicate parts of Trump’s legislative agenda. If Republicans retain control, the president would have a more favorable environment for advancing priorities through Congress. Those are possible consequences, not conclusions that can be drawn before votes are counted.

## Voters are also judging the president’s record

Midterm elections are often treated as a test of the party holding the White House. The Times reported that Trump was urging voters to see him as central to the election, even though his name is not on the ballot. Democrats are seeking to turn public concerns about the cost of living, the conflict involving Iran and the administration’s governing approach into support for their candidates.

National political messages can help mobilize voters, but individual races still have distinct dynamics. Candidates must respond to local concerns and persuade voters that they are the right choice for their state or district. The national mood may shape the environment, but it does not automatically determine every contest.

## Election administration and public trust remain in focus

The Times’ live report also described continuing concerns about election administration and public confidence in the process. Trump has repeatedly questioned aspects of election procedures, while recent federal actions have prompted concern among some observers about possible interference or voter intimidation. Election-monitoring organizations and volunteers planned to watch polling places.

Claims about election irregularities or interference should be distinguished from verified findings. The existence of political concern does not itself establish that voting has been disrupted or that any particular result is invalid. State and local election officials remain responsible for administering voting and counting ballots under applicable law. The final results will depend on those processes and subsequent certification.

## Early voting is a signal, not a result

The Times reported separately that more than 50 million Americans had already voted before Election Day. That figure indicates the scale of early participation, but it does not show how those voters cast their ballots. States differ in their voting rules and in the demographic information they release, making comparisons difficult.

Early-voting data can offer clues about turnout and enthusiasm, but it cannot substitute for actual vote totals. Campaigns may interpret the same indicators differently, particularly when party registration, geography and voting methods vary across states. The final balance of power will not be known until election officials count and report the votes.

The 2026 midterms will shape the remainder of Trump’s term by determining the legislative environment, the scope of congressional oversight and the politics of budget negotiations. But those implications remain contingent on the results. The final campaign messages show what each party hopes to achieve; only the ballots and certified counts will determine which party controls Congress.
