# 鲁宾天文台发现矮行星候选天体2026 ED138，仍需后续观测确认

## 海王星之外发现大型冰质天体
美国国家科学基金会与能源部联合资助的Vera C. Rubin Observatory公布了太阳系外缘的一项新发现：研究人员在早期观测数据中识别出编号为2026 ED138的海王星外天体，并将其列为矮行星候选。天体位于冥王星轨道之外，距离太阳约46个天文单位。研究团队认为，它可能是该区域已发现的较大、较明亮天体之一；但目前还不能称它为已确认的新矮行星。Rubin Observatory于10月27日公布发现，相关研究论文随后在arXiv发布。

## 直径估算约为600至1500公里
研究团队根据天体的亮度、距离和可能的表面反照率，估计其直径约为600至1500公里。论文给出的范围更精确，为612至1530公里，具体数值取决于研究人员采用的几何反照率假设。这个范围意味着ED138可能大到足以依靠自身引力形成近似球形，这是矮行星的重要条件之一。不过，天体大小目前仍是估算值，表面反射率尚未直接测定。不同反照率会让同样明亮的天体对应不同的实际直径，因此科学家仍需要进一步观测来确定其物理性质。

## 十多年的影像帮助确认轨道
Rubin团队成员Matthew Holman在分析早期数据时发现了ED138。研究人员随后重新检查观测目录和图像，补充找到了更多记录。Rubin望远镜在2026年3月4日至7月11日期间对该天体进行了30次观测，分布在20个夜晚；研究人员还从更早的档案图像中找到追溯记录，最早可回溯到2016年，使轨道观测跨度超过十年。论文称，ED138的轨道半长轴约为43.09个天文单位，轨道倾角约为24.4度。它靠近银河系平面，背景恒星密集，可能是过去的巡天项目没有发现它的原因之一。

## 为什么候选身份还不能变成正式分类
按照国际天文学联合会的定义，矮行星必须绕太阳运行，具有足够质量让自身引力把它塑造成近似球形，同时又没有清除轨道附近的其他天体。ED138已被确认是海王星外天体，但研究人员尚未完成确认其直径、密度和形状所需的测量。Rubin Observatory表示，需要红外后续观测来进一步约束尺寸并确认其性质；Reuters报道也提到，研究人员希望利用更长波长观测，例如智利的ALMA阵列，测量天体自身发出的热辐射。不同观测手段将帮助研究团队减少反照率带来的不确定性。

## Rubin巡天或将带来更多远方天体
如果ED138最终被确认为矮行星，它将加入目前国际天文学联合会认可的五颗矮行星：冥王星、谷神星、阋神星、鸟神星和妊神星。它也将成为Rubin Observatory早期太阳系巡天成果中的重要案例。该天文台计划持续开展为期十年的南天巡天，反复拍摄大片天空并通过算法寻找缓慢移动的天体。研究论文认为，银河系平面附近仍可能藏有尚未发现的明亮海王星外天体。此次发现的重要意义不仅在于候选天体本身，也在于新一代巡天设备能够从过去难以处理的密集星场中识别远距离小天体。

# Rubin Observatory Finds 2026 ED138, a Large Dwarf-Planet Candidate Beyond Pluto

## A large icy object appears in the outer Solar System
The NSF–DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory has announced a discovery in the distant Solar System: researchers identified a trans-Neptunian object provisionally designated 2026 ED138 in the observatory’s early data and classified it as a dwarf-planet candidate. The object lies beyond Pluto’s orbit, at a distance of about 46 astronomical units from the Sun. The research team says it may be among the largest and brightest objects found in that region, but it has not been confirmed as a dwarf planet. Rubin Observatory announced the discovery on October 27, and the scientific team subsequently described the object in a paper posted to arXiv.

## Its estimated diameter ranges from about 600 to 1,500 kilometers
Researchers estimate that ED138 is roughly 600 to 1,500 kilometers across, based on its brightness, distance and assumed surface reflectivity, or albedo. The research paper gives a more precise range of 612 to 1,530 kilometers, depending on the geometric albedo assumed. That size range suggests the object may be massive enough for its own gravity to pull it into a nearly spherical shape, one of the key requirements for dwarf-planet status. The diameter remains an estimate, however, because the surface reflectivity has not yet been measured directly. A smaller, more reflective object can appear as bright as a larger, darker one, so additional observations are needed to determine its physical properties.

## More than a decade of images helped establish its orbit
Rubin team member Matthew Holman found ED138 while analyzing early survey data. Researchers then searched the observatory’s catalogs and images for additional detections. Rubin observed the object 30 times on 20 nights between March 4 and July 11, 2026. Archival images from other surveys provided earlier detections, extending the observational record back to 2016 and giving the team an orbital arc of more than a decade. The paper reports a barycentric orbital semimajor axis of about 43.09 astronomical units and an inclination of about 24.4 degrees. ED138 lies close to the plane of the Milky Way, where dense fields of background stars make moving objects difficult to distinguish. That crowded region may help explain why earlier surveys missed it.

## Why the candidate is not yet a confirmed dwarf planet
Under the International Astronomical Union’s definition, a dwarf planet orbits the Sun and has enough mass for its own gravity to make it nearly round, but it has not cleared other objects from its orbital neighborhood. ED138 is established as a trans-Neptunian object, but researchers have not yet completed the measurements needed to confirm its size, density and shape. Rubin Observatory says infrared follow-up observations are needed to constrain the object’s size and confirm its nature. Reuters also reported that researchers hope to use observations at longer wavelengths, including measurements from Chile’s ALMA array, to detect heat emitted by the object. Such observations can help reduce the uncertainty created by the unknown surface reflectivity.

## Rubin’s survey could reveal more distant objects
If ED138 is confirmed as a dwarf planet, it would join the five currently recognized by the International Astronomical Union: Pluto, Ceres, Eris, Haumea and Makemake. It would also become an important early result from Rubin Observatory’s Solar System survey. The observatory is beginning a decade-long effort to repeatedly image the southern sky and use algorithms to identify slowly moving objects. The research paper suggests that additional bright trans-Neptunian objects may remain undiscovered, particularly in or near crowded regions of the Milky Way. The significance of the finding is therefore not limited to one possible new dwarf planet. It also demonstrates how modern wide-field surveys can find distant objects hidden in star fields that earlier searches struggled to examine.
