Saudi Aramco第三季度净利润增长34.5%，能源设施遇袭背景下维持出口

Saudi Aramco公布2026年第三季度业绩，净利润达到362.5亿美元，较上年同期增长34.5%。Reuters和Associated Press报道，利润增长主要受较高油价、炼油利润改善，以及出售马来西亚PRefChem合资企业股权带来的收益推动。与此同时，美国与伊朗战争及针对能源基础设施的袭击影响了产量和出口路线。公司称，East-West Pipeline在遭遇干扰后恢复运作，继续帮助原油经红海出口。

## 净利润与调整后利润不同

Aramco第三季度净利润为362.5亿美元，高于去年同期的269亿美元。若剔除PRefChem股权出售等项目，调整后净利润为312亿美元，同比增长9%。Reuters指出，这一调整后利润高于Aramco提供的12名分析师一致预期306.6亿美元。报道中34.5%的净利润增幅与9%的调整后净利润增幅口径不同，不能混为一谈。

## 油价上涨抵消部分产量下降

Reuters报道，Aramco第三季度实现的平均原油价格为每桶95.20美元，高于去年同期的70.10美元。同期公司总烃类产量平均为每日1105万桶油当量，低于去年同期的1330万桶油当量。Saudi Arabia 9月原油产量约为每日800万桶，低于战争前略高于每日1000万桶的水平。油价上涨改善收入，但产量下降仍反映出供应环境承压。

## 管道成为替代出口通道

由于霍尔木兹海峡航运风险上升，Aramco利用East-West Pipeline将原油输送至红海沿岸的Yanbu港。AP报道，该管道于9月10日遭到袭击，之后恢复输送。Reuters称，Aramco第三季度供应可靠性为97.8%，低于去年同期的99.9%，公司表示通过管线多线配置恢复西部装载作业。该管道帮助公司在传统海湾出口路线受阻时维持部分出口。

## 股息维持在219亿美元

Aramco董事会宣布第三季度基础股息为219亿美元，同比增长3.5%，计划在第四季度支付。公司还表示，Zuluf原油项目已开始生产，Jafurah天然气项目第二阶段及其他上游项目仍按计划推进。Reuters称，Zuluf项目预计在2026年底前达到每日最多60万桶原油处理能力；这一目标是项目设计能力，不应理解为目前已经达到的产量。

## 高利润背后仍有地缘风险

Aramco业绩显示，高油价和炼油利润可以部分抵消供应中断的影响，但不能消除基础设施安全、运输和产量风险。公司正在探索更多海外储存能力和替代出口路线。对于全球能源市场，关键变量仍包括霍尔木兹海峡航运状况、能源设施是否继续遭袭、油价变化以及沙特实际产量恢复速度。

Saudi Aramco's Third-Quarter Net Profit Rises 34.5% Despite Attacks on Energy Infrastructure

Saudi Aramco reported third-quarter 2026 net profit of $36.25 billion, a 34.5% increase from the same period a year earlier. Reuters and The Associated Press reported that stronger oil prices, improved refining margins and a gain from the sale of the company's stake in the Malaysian PRefChem joint venture helped lift earnings. At the same time, the US-Iran war and attacks on energy infrastructure affected production and export routes. Aramco said its East-West Pipeline continued to provide an alternative route for moving crude to the Red Sea after disruptions.

## Net profit and adjusted income measure different things

Aramco's third-quarter net profit reached $36.25 billion, compared with $26.9 billion in the year-earlier quarter. Adjusted net income, which excludes certain items including the gain from the PRefChem transaction, was $31.2 billion, up 9% year over year. Reuters said the adjusted figure exceeded the median analyst consensus of $30.66 billion shared by Aramco from 12 analysts.

The reported 34.5% increase in net profit and the 9% increase in adjusted net income are not contradictory. They use different accounting measures. The gain associated with the disposal of the Malaysian joint-venture stake contributed to reported net income, while adjusted income is intended to provide a view of underlying operating performance after specified adjustments.

## Higher oil prices offset part of the production decline

Reuters reported that Aramco's average realized crude price was $95.20 per barrel in the third quarter, compared with $70.10 a year earlier. Total hydrocarbon production averaged 11.05 million barrels of oil equivalent per day, down from 13.3 million in the year-earlier period. Saudi Arabia's crude output was about 8 million barrels per day in September, compared with slightly more than 10 million barrels per day before the war, according to secondary sources cited by OPEC.

Higher prices supported revenue even as production volumes declined. The figures also show why headline profit growth should not be read as evidence that the disruption had no effect. Aramco's earnings benefited from a favorable price environment, while the lower production figures reflected constraints on supply.

## The pipeline provides an alternative export route

As risks to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz increased, Aramco used its East-West Pipeline to move crude toward Yanbu on the Red Sea coast. The Associated Press reported that the pipeline was attacked on September 10 and later resumed operations. Reuters said Aramco's supply reliability was 97.8% during the quarter, down from 99.9% a year earlier. The company said it restored western-region loadings using the pipeline's multiple-line configuration after disruptions.

The pipeline is strategically important because it gives Saudi Arabia an alternative route to export terminals outside the Persian Gulf. It does not remove all shipping and security risks, but it can reduce the country's dependence on tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz during periods of heightened tension.

## The company declared a $21.9 billion dividend

Aramco's board declared a third-quarter base dividend of $21.9 billion, up 3.5% year over year and scheduled for payment in the fourth quarter. The company also said production had begun at its Zuluf crude project and that the second phase of the Jafurah gas project remained on track. Reuters reported that Zuluf is expected to process up to 600,000 barrels per day by the end of 2026. That is a project capacity target, not a statement that the facility is already producing at that level.

## Strong earnings do not remove geopolitical exposure

Aramco's results show how higher oil prices and stronger refining margins can offset part of the financial impact of supply disruption. They do not eliminate the risks posed by attacks on energy infrastructure, shipping constraints or lower output. The company said it is exploring additional overseas storage capacity and alternative export routes.

For global energy markets, the important variables remain the security of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, the condition of energy facilities, oil-price movements and the pace at which Saudi production can recover. Aramco's quarterly earnings were strong, but the operating environment remains exposed to geopolitical developments.
