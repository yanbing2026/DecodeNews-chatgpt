欧洲至少7班航班收到危险物品警报：机场短暂受扰，搜查均未发现可疑物

欧洲多国11月3日接连收到针对客运航班的危险物品警报，至少7班航班受到影响，涉及波兰、意大利、西班牙和保加利亚。机场和警方启动安全程序，部分航班改降、跑道暂时关闭或机场短暂停运。Reuters和美联社报道，所有受检飞机均未发现可疑物，也没有人员受伤。有关部门尚未公布警报发送者身份，也没有证据确认这些事件由同一组织策划。

## 七班航班分布在四个国家

Reuters统计，周二共有至少7班航班收到警报：两班飞往华沙、两班飞往索非亚、两班飞往罗马，以及一班飞往西班牙穆尔西亚。保加利亚交通部门向Reuters确认，飞往索非亚的两班飞机分别来自意大利贝加莫和爱尔兰都柏林；早些时候有报道称其中一班来自以色列，后续已被更正。

波兰方面，一架LOT航空公司从伦敦希思罗飞往华沙肖邦机场的航班，因警报改降华沙莫德林机场。莫德林机场暂时关闭，飞机和乘客接受检查，未发现可疑物后机场重新开放。另一架从哥本哈根飞往华沙的SAS航班正常降落肖邦机场，机场曾关闭一条跑道进行安全检查。

意大利方面，两架Ryanair航班分别从里斯本和都柏林飞往罗马菲乌米奇诺机场。两架飞机、乘客和行李均接受检查，未发现可疑物；其中都柏林航班在降落前由意大利空军战机护航。ANSA和美联社均报道，机场安全检查结束后恢复正常运行。

## 西班牙和保加利亚航班也接受检查

一架从英国曼彻斯特飞往西班牙穆尔西亚的Ryanair航班收到警报后安全降落，机上121名乘客和机组人员接受检查。西班牙警方检查飞机和行李，没有发现可疑物。穆尔西亚机场部分区域一度受到限制，完成检查后恢复运行。

保加利亚交通部长表示，两架分别来自贝加莫和都柏林的航班降落索非亚后接受反恐部门检查，均未发现异常。Reuters称，Wizz Air运营的贝加莫航班载有176名乘客，Ryanair运营的都柏林航班载有186名乘客。两班飞机都在索非亚正常降落，没有因警报改降。

## 周一布达佩斯事件属于另一宗警报

周一，一架从伦敦卢顿飞往索非亚的Wizz Air航班在飞行途中收到警报，随后改降匈牙利布达佩斯。匈牙利空军派出Gripen战机护航，飞机安全降落，检查未发现异常。这宗事件发生在周二的7班航班警报之前，应与周二统计分开计算。

美联社指出，欧洲上个月也发生过类似事件，一架从特拉维夫飞往维也纳的航班曾因安全警报在保加利亚紧急降落。连续发生的警报提高了机场安全部门的警戒程度，但目前公开信息尚不能证明这些事件之间存在直接联系。

## 警报来源与是否有关联仍在调查

意大利媒体报道，罗马两班航班的警报源自匿名电子邮件。保加利亚官员表示，警方正努力识别警报发送者。Reuters指出，没有组织宣称对此负责，各国官员也没有公开指认幕后责任方。虽然有安全分析人士将此类低成本警报视为可能扰乱欧洲交通的手段，但这并不等于已经证实存在跨国协调行动。

## 机场恢复运行不代表调查结束

截至11月3日的报道，各地搜查没有发现可疑物，受影响航班均已安全降落，机场也陆续恢复正常运行。事件仍可能造成延误、改降、旅客安检和机场运营成本上升。航空公司和机场后续仍需配合警方调查警报来源，并评估是否需要调整安全程序。

At Least Seven European Flights Receive Security Alerts; Searches Find No Suspicious Items

At least seven passenger flights across Europe received security alerts on November 3, affecting airports in Poland, Italy, Spain and Bulgaria. Airport and police authorities activated security procedures, leading to diversions, temporary runway closures and short interruptions to airport operations. Reuters and The Associated Press reported that searches found no suspicious items on the affected aircraft and that no one was injured. Authorities had not identified who sent the alerts, and there was no confirmed evidence that the incidents were coordinated by a single group.

## Seven flights were affected in four countries

Reuters counted at least seven flights targeted by alerts on Tuesday: two bound for Warsaw, two for Sofia, two for Rome and one for Murcia in Spain. Bulgaria's transport ministry later confirmed to Reuters that the two Sofia-bound aircraft were travelling from Bergamo, Italy, and Dublin, Ireland. Earlier reports had incorrectly suggested that one of the planes had come from Israel; that information was subsequently corrected.

In Poland, a LOT flight from London's Heathrow Airport to Warsaw Chopin Airport was diverted to Warsaw Modlin after an alert. Modlin temporarily closed while the aircraft and passengers were checked. No suspicious item was found, and the airport reopened. A separate SAS flight from Copenhagen landed as scheduled at Chopin Airport, where one runway was temporarily closed for security checks.

In Italy, two Ryanair flights from Lisbon and Dublin were searched after arriving at Rome's Fiumicino Airport. Aircraft, passengers and baggage were checked, with no suspicious items found. The Dublin flight was escorted by Italian Air Force fighter jets before landing. ANSA and the AP reported that airport operations returned to normal after the searches were completed.

## Flights in Spain and Bulgaria were also inspected

A Ryanair flight from Manchester in the United Kingdom to Murcia in Spain landed safely after an alert. The 121 passengers and crew members were checked, and Spanish police inspected the aircraft and baggage. No suspicious items were found. Parts of Murcia airport were temporarily restricted before normal operations resumed.

Bulgaria's transport minister said two flights from Bergamo and Dublin were inspected by counterterrorism officers after landing in Sofia. No suspicious objects were found. Reuters reported that the Bergamo flight, operated by Wizz Air, carried 176 passengers, while the Ryanair flight from Dublin carried 186. Both aircraft landed in Sofia as scheduled and were not diverted because of the alerts.

## Monday's Budapest diversion was a separate incident

On Monday, a Wizz Air flight from London Luton to Sofia received an alert while in the air and was diverted to Budapest, Hungary. Hungarian Gripen fighter jets escorted the aircraft to a safe landing. Searches found no suspicious items. That incident occurred before Tuesday's seven flight alerts and should be counted separately.

The Associated Press noted that Europe had also seen a similar incident last month, when a flight from Tel Aviv to Vienna made an emergency landing in Bulgaria following a security alert. The repeated warnings have raised the security posture at airports, but publicly available information does not establish a direct connection among the incidents.

## The source of the alerts and any possible links remain under investigation

Italian media reported that anonymous emails triggered the alerts involving the two Rome-bound flights. Bulgarian officials said authorities were working to identify the sender. Reuters reported that no group had claimed responsibility and that officials had not publicly blamed any actor. Some security analysts have suggested that low-cost alerts could be used to disrupt European transport, but that does not establish that a coordinated cross-border operation took place.

## Airports have reopened, but investigations continue

As of reports published on November 3, searches had found no suspicious items, the affected aircraft had landed safely and airports had resumed normal operations. The incidents could nevertheless cause delays, diversions, additional passenger screening and higher operating costs. Airlines and airport authorities are expected to continue cooperating with police investigations into the source of the alerts and to review whether security procedures need to change.
