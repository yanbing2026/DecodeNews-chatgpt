# 徒步28年、行程超过3.1万英里，Karl Bushby回到英国与母亲团聚

英国冒险家 Karl Bushby 完成了从南美洲返回英国 Hull 的长途徒步旅程，结束了持续28年的环球行走挑战。Bushby 于1998年从智利 Punta Arenas 出发，目标是在不使用交通工具推进旅程的情况下，一路步行回到家乡。11月2日，他抵达母亲 Angela Bushby 位于 Hull 的家门前，两人实现多年未见后的团聚。

## 从智利出发的漫长旅程

BBC 报道，Bushby 于1998年11月从 Punta Arenas 出发，当时29岁。他为旅程设定了两条规则：在挑战完成前不返回英国，也不使用交通工具来推进路线。经过28年，他走过超过31,000英里（约50,000公里），穿越四大洲，最终回到英格兰东北部的 Hull。

## 穿越丛林、冰海和英吉利海峡

旅程中的关键路段包括穿越 Darién Gap 的密集丛林、跨越冰封的 Bering Strait，以及最近游过英吉利海峡。Bushby 还在途中经历不同国家的边境和通行限制。BBC 报道称，他在抵达 Hull 前受到当地居民欢迎，随后走完最后几英里，回到母亲在 Sutton Park 的家。

## 母子在家门口重逢

Bushby 的母亲 Angela 在家中等待他。两人团聚时，她以“Where have you been?”打趣儿子。Bushby 对 BBC 表示，回家感觉“不可思议”，并称这是28年旅程的终点。母亲则表示，儿子终于回家让她感到宽慰。报道指出，母子此前已有十年没有见面。

## 结束旅程后适应普通生活

Bushby 在团聚后的记者会上表示，他准备结束这段行走挑战，并说自己“要把靴子挂起来”。他也坦言，回归日常生活后可能需要时间适应。长期以来，冒险者身份构成了他生活的重要部分，旅程结束后，他需要重新思考下一阶段的人生安排。

## 未来计划包括纪录片和新书

BBC 报道称，Bushby 正与德国电影制作人 Werner Herzog 合作一部纪录片，并在准备第二本关于旅行经历的书。他表示自己有很多故事要讲。旅程的完成并不意味着所有相关工作已经结束，沿途经历仍将通过纪录片、写作和采访继续被整理和讲述。

这次归家也得到家人和当地社区的欢迎。Bushby 的儿子 Adam、孙女、父亲和兄弟在他进入 East Yorkshire 时迎接他。对于一个从29岁出发、在外行走28年的人而言，回到母亲家门前不仅是地理上的终点，也意味着长期自我设定的规则终于完成。

# Karl Bushby Completes a 28-Year, 31,000-Mile Journey and Reunites With His Mother in Hull

British adventurer Karl Bushby has completed his long journey on foot from South America to his home city of Hull, England, ending a challenge that lasted 28 years. Bushby set out from Punta Arenas, Chile, in November 1998, with the goal of returning home without using transport to advance his route. On November 2, 2026, he reached his mother Angela’s home in Hull and reunited with her after years apart.

## A journey that began in Chile

The BBC reported that Bushby left Punta Arenas in November 1998, when he was 29. He established two rules for the expedition: he would not return to the United Kingdom until the journey was complete, and he would not use transport to move himself forward along the route.

After 28 years, he had traveled more than 31,000 miles, or about 50,000 kilometers, across four continents. The journey ended in Hull, in northeastern England, where he had grown up. His return marked the completion of a challenge that had occupied much of his adult life.

## Through jungles, frozen waters and the English Channel

Bushby’s route included several demanding crossings. The BBC highlighted his passage through the dense jungle of the Darién Gap, his crossing of the frozen Bering Strait and his more recent swim across the English Channel. The journey also required him to navigate borders and travel restrictions across multiple countries.

Before reaching his mother’s home, Bushby was welcomed by crowds in Hull. He crossed the Humber Bridge into East Yorkshire and then completed the final miles through the city. His arrival brought the expedition to its intended destination after nearly three decades.

## A reunion at his mother’s front door

Bushby’s mother, Angela, waited for him at the family home in Sutton Park. When they finally met, she greeted him with the question, “Where have you been?” The line captured the long absence with humor, while the reunion itself was emotional.

Bushby told the BBC that being home felt surreal and that it marked the end of a 28-year journey. His mother said she was relieved to have him back. The BBC reported that the two had not seen each other for a decade before the reunion.

## Adjusting to life after the expedition

At a press conference after arriving home, Bushby said he was hanging up his boots. He also acknowledged that returning to ordinary life could be difficult. He had spent much of his adult life identifying himself through the expedition, and the end of the journey creates a new challenge: adjusting to a life without the daily demands of moving toward the next destination.

Bushby said he was still processing the experience and expected to struggle in the coming days and weeks as he adapted. The end of the physical journey does not necessarily mean an immediate end to the work of making sense of it.

## A documentary and another book are planned

The BBC reported that Bushby is working with German filmmaker Werner Herzog on a documentary and is preparing a second book about his travels. He said he had a story to tell. The expedition’s experiences may therefore continue to reach audiences through film, writing and interviews.

Family members also gathered to welcome him back. The BBC reported that his son Adam, his granddaughter, his father and his brothers met him as he returned to East Yorkshire. Bushby had never previously met his 12-year-old granddaughter.

The journey’s completion is both a geographical and personal milestone. Bushby left Chile at 29 and returned to his mother’s home at 56. After more than 31,000 miles on foot and 28 years away, he has fulfilled the rules he set for himself. What comes next will involve a different kind of transition: turning a lifetime of travel into a story and rebuilding a daily routine at home.
