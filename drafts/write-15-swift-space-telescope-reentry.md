# NASA Swift太空望远镜预计11月4日重返大气层，地面人员受伤风险约为二千一

## 运行近22年的望远镜即将再入
NASA表示，Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory预计将于11月4日重返地球大气层。这颗太空望远镜发射时已是多波段天文观测任务的重要设备，长期用于研究宇宙中强烈的爆发现象。NASA称，Swift已在轨运行近22年，随着轨道逐渐降低，预计将在本周进入大气层。此次再入不是受控着陆，具体时间和残骸落点仍有不确定性。

## 再入时间仍有较大误差范围
根据NASA 11月2日公布的信息，美国太空军当时预测Swift可能在美国东部时间11月4日凌晨约3时30分再入大气层，但时间误差范围约为11小时。预测会随着轨道观测更新而调整，因此不能把单一时间点理解为确定的撞击时间。NASA和太空军将继续监测轨道，并根据新的数据更新预测。

## 大部分结构预计会在大气层烧毁
NASA表示，Swift重量约3,200磅，预计大部分结构会在再入过程中燃烧，但部分较坚固的组件可能存活并到达地面或海面。由于卫星轨道倾角较低，可能出现残骸的区域受到纬度范围限制，约在南纬20.6度至北纬20.6度之间。具体残骸位置取决于实际再入时间和轨道状态，目前无法精确预测。

## NASA估算人员受伤风险很低
NASA给出的估算是，残骸造成地面人员受伤的概率约为二千一分之一。这个数字描述的是人员受伤风险，不是残骸必然落在人口密集地区的概率。NASA强调，大部分航天器会在大气层中烧毁，仍可能存活的部件数量和落点存在不确定性。公众不应把可能再入区域理解为某个具体城市或地点将会受到撞击。

## 卫星再入风险成为长期议题
Swift的再入事件也反映了卫星寿命结束后的轨道安全问题。随着近地轨道活动增加，更多航天器需要在任务结束后安全离轨。NASA和美国太空军持续跟踪Swift，是为了缩小再入时间和可能区域的预测范围。后续应关注官方更新，而不是依赖未经证实的社交媒体落点推测。

# NASA’s Swift Space Telescope Expected to Reenter on November 4, With a Low Risk to People on the Ground

## A nearly 22-year mission is nearing reentry
NASA says the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory is expected to reenter Earth’s atmosphere on November 4. The spacecraft was designed as a multiwavelength observatory to study some of the most powerful explosions in the universe. After nearly 22 years in orbit, its altitude has declined and it is expected to return through the atmosphere this week. The reentry is not a controlled landing, and both the timing and the location of any surviving debris remain uncertain.

## The predicted time still has a wide margin
In an update published November 2, NASA said the U.S. Space Force forecast a reentry at approximately 3:30 a.m. Eastern on November 4, with an uncertainty of about 11 hours. The prediction can change as orbital tracking data are updated. The stated time should therefore not be treated as a precise moment when debris will reach the ground. NASA and the Space Force said they would continue monitoring the spacecraft and revising the forecast as needed.

## Most of the spacecraft is expected to burn up
NASA describes Swift as weighing about 3,200 pounds. Most of the observatory is expected to burn up as it passes through the atmosphere, although some components may survive and reach Earth’s surface or the ocean. Because of the spacecraft’s relatively low orbital inclination, the possible reentry region is limited to latitudes between 20.6 degrees south and 20.6 degrees north. The actual location of any surviving fragments will depend on the precise reentry time and the spacecraft’s orbital conditions.

## NASA estimates a low risk of harm
NASA estimates the chance of someone on Earth being harmed by surviving debris at approximately one in 2,100. That is an estimate of the risk of harm to a person, not a prediction that debris will strike a populated area. The agency expects most of the spacecraft to burn up, while acknowledging that some components could survive. The number and location of any fragments cannot be predicted precisely at this stage. The possible latitude band should not be interpreted as a forecast for a specific city or community.

## The event highlights the long-term issue of satellite disposal
Swift’s return also illustrates the growing challenge of managing spacecraft at the end of their missions. As activity in low Earth orbit increases, more satellites must be monitored and safely removed from orbit when their missions end. NASA and the Space Force are tracking Swift to narrow the expected reentry window and potential region. The most reliable information will come from official updates as the spacecraft approaches reentry, not from unverified social-media claims about a specific impact location.
