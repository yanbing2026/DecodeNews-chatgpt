# Grok Bot使用额度收紧，马斯克承认服务成本相对收入“难以持续”

SpaceX旗下AI代理服务 Grok Bot 的用户近日报告每周使用额度快速耗尽。SpaceX CEO Elon Musk 在社交平台 X 上回应称，提供 Grok Bot 的成本相对于收入“难以持续”，并确认额度变化并非系统故障。TechCrunch 11月2日报道，部分用户即使没有明显使用服务，额度也从约50%升至100%。公司尚未公布新的使用额度规则。

## 用户发现额度异常耗尽

TechCrunch 报道称，部分 Grok Bot 用户在万圣节周末发现，每周使用额度突然达到上限。有人在 X 上表示，自己的使用量在没有相应操作的情况下从约50%升至100%。Musk 随后回应用户投诉，称服务成本相对于收入过高。这一表态表明，额度收紧与服务运营成本有关，而不是简单的界面显示问题。

## 马斯克承认成本与收入失衡

Musk 在11月1日的 X 回复中写道，提供 Grok Bot 的成本相对于收入“unsustainably high”。TechCrunch 指出，Musk 没有在自己的主信息流中详细解释具体调整方式，也没有公布未来订阅用户的额度将如何变化。Business Insider 报道称，SpaceX此前表示 Grok Bot 已拥有超过80万名付费周活跃用户，但使用额度仍按不同订阅等级设置。

## 代理式AI服务计算成本较高

Grok Bot 被定位为可以操作虚拟计算机、替用户完成任务的AI代理。此类服务可能需要模型多轮推理、调用工具并持续执行任务，资源消耗与一次普通问答不同。The Independent 报道指出，代理服务需要运行多个协同AI步骤，因此提供服务的成本较高。对于运营商而言，用户使用量增长并不必然意味着单位服务成本下降。

## SpaceX披露AI业务财务数据

TechCrunch 报道称，SpaceX上个月的一份文件表示，AI业务占公司收入的52%，其更名后的“Super Intelligence”部门实现4.31亿美元利润，但其中包括与收购 Cursor 有关的一次性成本。报道还提到，相关收购产生了12亿美元的一次性费用。上述数字来自公司文件及媒体报道，不能简单理解为 Grok Bot 单一产品的利润或成本。

## 额度和商业模式仍待明确

Musk 尚未说明不同订阅等级的用户额度会如何调整，也没有公布具体价格或服务限制的变化。Grok Bot 的案例反映出代理式AI产品在扩大使用规模时面临的经济问题：复杂任务可能带来更高推理和工具调用成本，而订阅收入未必同步增长。用户能否获得稳定额度，将取决于公司如何调整服务能力、定价和资源分配。

# Grok Bot Usage Limits Tighten as Elon Musk Says Service Costs Are “Unsustainably High”

Users of SpaceX’s Grok Bot AI agent have reported that their weekly usage allowances were being exhausted unexpectedly. SpaceX CEO Elon Musk acknowledged on X that the cost of serving the product was “unsustainably high” relative to revenue and said the issue was not a glitch. TechCrunch reported on November 2 that some users saw their weekly usage limits rise from around 50% to 100% even when they had not knowingly used the service. The company has not yet explained the full effect on subscribers.

## Users report unexpected usage-limit changes

TechCrunch said some Grok Bot users noticed over Halloween weekend that their weekly allowances had suddenly reached their maximum. Users posted on X that their usage had moved from around 50% to 100% without corresponding activity. Musk responded to a complaint and said the change was not caused by a technical glitch.

The reports prompted questions about how the service measures usage and whether the company had changed its limits. Musk’s response connected the restrictions to the economics of operating the product, rather than describing them as a temporary display error.

## Musk acknowledges a gap between costs and revenue

In a reply posted on November 1, Musk wrote that “the cost of serving Grok Bot was unsustainably high relative to revenue.” TechCrunch reported that he did not provide a detailed explanation of the changes on his main X feed or specify how usage allowances would be adjusted for subscribers.

Business Insider reported that SpaceX had said Grok Bot had more than 800,000 paid weekly users. The service offers different subscription tiers with different usage limits. The user complaints suggest that the amount of work available under those tiers may be changing, but the company has not published a complete revised schedule.

## Agentic AI can require substantial computing resources

Grok Bot is positioned as an AI agent that can use a virtual computer to carry out tasks for users. Such systems may need to reason through multiple steps, call tools and continue working over a period of time. That can consume more computing resources than a single conversational response.

The Independent reported that personal AI agents can involve coordinated systems and complicated tasks, making them resource-intensive to operate. The economics of these products depend not only on how many people subscribe, but also on how much work each user performs and how expensive each task is to complete.

## SpaceX has disclosed figures for its AI business

TechCrunch reported that a SpaceX filing from the previous month said AI accounted for 52% of company income. The company’s renamed “Super Intelligence” division recorded a profit of $431 million, although the report also described $1.2 billion in one-time costs related to the acquisition of Cursor.

Those figures should not be treated as the standalone financial results of Grok Bot. They concern the wider AI business and include acquisition-related expenses. The public information does not provide a detailed product-level breakdown showing how much revenue Grok Bot generates or how much it costs to serve an individual subscriber.

## The pricing and usage model remains unclear

Musk has not announced how the usage limits will change across subscription tiers, whether prices will be adjusted or what level of access customers can expect. The company’s response points to a wider challenge for agentic AI services: more usage can increase revenue, but it can also increase the cost of inference, tool use and task execution.

For subscribers, the immediate uncertainty is whether their weekly allowances will remain predictable. For SpaceX, the issue is whether pricing and limits can bring the cost of providing the service closer to the revenue it generates. Until the company provides further details, the exact impact on users remains unknown.
