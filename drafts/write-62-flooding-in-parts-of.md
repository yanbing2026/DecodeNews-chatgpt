# 美国西南部暴雨引发亚利桑那和科罗拉多撤离，洪水风险持续

一场强烈风暴系统在11月1日至2日给美国西南部带来持续强降雨，亚利桑那州 Sedona 和科罗拉多州 Pagosa Springs 等地发布撤离命令。此前一周刚遭遇严重洪灾的地区再次面临山洪风险，河流水位和道路积水威胁居民及救援人员。美国国家气象部门警告，已经饱和的地面难以吸收更多降雨，短时间内可能出现新的洪水。

## Sedona部分地区发布撤离命令

The New York Times 报道，Sedona 市政府在周日发布部分地区撤离命令。当地消防部门和警方协助居民离开受威胁区域，并提醒民众不要穿越有流水的道路。亚利桑那州 Flagstaff 也出现持续降雨、道路积水和排水系统负荷过重的情况。当地政府要求居民关注官方警报，避免进入低洼地带。

## 科罗拉多Pagosa Springs面临河水上涨

美联社11月2日报道，Pagosa Springs 的居民正在为 San Juan River 水位可能达到历史高位做准备。当地部分居民被要求撤离，救援人员协助居民转移。科罗拉多州其他地区也发生道路被洪水淹没和人员救援行动。强降雨在短时间内集中落下，使河道和排水系统承受巨大压力。

## 前一轮洪灾增加地面风险

气象部门指出，部分地区在本轮风暴前已经经历严重洪灾，土壤含水量很高，山坡、峡谷、排水沟和野火烧毁区域尤其容易发生快速径流。美国天气预报中心警告，进一步降雨可能迅速汇集，形成突发性山洪。即使降雨减弱，水位上涨和道路受损仍可能持续影响当地。

## 救援和疏散仍在进行

美联社报道，多个地区的居民被迫离开住所，急流救援队持续执行任务。科罗拉多州 Durango 附近的 Junction Creek Mobile Home Park 发生洪水，救援人员协助居民撤离。地方政府还需要检查道路、桥梁和供水设施，洪水退去后清理和修复工作可能持续数周。

## 天气风险与伤亡统计需区分

美联社11月2日更新报道提到，洪水中至少有一人死亡，警方怀疑另一起死亡可能与天气有关。相关死亡事件并非本文的主要报道焦点，且第二起事件仍待确认。各地政府继续根据河流水位和降雨预报调整撤离范围。居民应遵循当地紧急警报，不应尝试驾车穿越积水道路。

这场风暴再次暴露出美国西南部干旱地区在连续强降雨下的脆弱性。短时强降雨、饱和土壤和复杂地形叠加，使洪水风险持续存在。尽管部分地区雨势开始减弱，撤离、救援和基础设施检查仍在继续。

# Heavy Rain Triggers Evacuations in Arizona and Colorado as Flood Risk Persists Across the Southwest

A powerful storm system brought heavy rain to the U.S. Southwest on November 1 and 2, prompting evacuation orders in parts of Arizona and Colorado. Communities including Sedona and Pagosa Springs faced renewed flooding after severe inundation in the region just over a week earlier. Forecasters warned that already saturated ground could not absorb much additional rainfall, increasing the risk of flash floods, dangerous road conditions and further evacuations.

## Evacuation orders issued in parts of Sedona

The New York Times reported that Sedona, Arizona, issued evacuation orders for some areas on Sunday. The city’s fire department and police worked to move people away from rising water. Officials warned residents not to cross roads where water was flowing.

Flagstaff also experienced prolonged rainfall, flooding on local roads and storm drains overwhelmed by runoff. Authorities urged residents to monitor official alerts and avoid low-lying areas where water could rise quickly.

## Pagosa Springs prepares for rising river levels

The Associated Press reported on November 2 that residents in Pagosa Springs, Colorado, were preparing for the San Juan River to rise to potentially historic levels. Some residents were told to evacuate, and emergency responders assisted people leaving threatened areas.

Other parts of Colorado also experienced flooded roads and rescue operations. Heavy rainfall concentrated over a short period placed pressure on rivers, drainage systems and local infrastructure.

## Earlier flooding increased the danger

The Weather Prediction Center warned that additional rainfall on already saturated ground could quickly run off. The greatest risk was expected in parts of Arizona, southern Utah, southwestern Colorado and western New Mexico. Steep terrain, slot canyons, washes and areas affected by previous wildfires were especially vulnerable to rapid runoff.

Some communities were still recovering from destructive flooding the previous week. That left less capacity for the ground and drainage systems to absorb another round of heavy rain. Even after rainfall begins to ease, river levels and damaged roads can remain dangerous.

## Rescue and evacuation operations continue

The Associated Press reported that hundreds of people were forced to evacuate across the region and that swift-water rescue teams remained busy. Near Durango, Colorado, flooding at Junction Creek Mobile Home Park prompted evacuations, with responders helping residents leave the area.

Local authorities also face the task of inspecting roads, bridges and water systems. Cleanup and repairs may take weeks in some locations, depending on the damage and whether further rainfall occurs.

## Weather impacts and casualty reports should be distinguished

An AP update on November 2 said at least one person had died in floodwaters and that police suspected a second death might be weather-related. The second case had not been confirmed as a flood fatality. Those reports are separate from the main focus of this story, which is the evacuation orders and continuing flood risk.

Officials may adjust evacuation zones as river levels and forecasts change. Residents are being urged to follow local emergency alerts and never drive through floodwater. The combination of heavy rain, saturated soil and difficult terrain means that the danger can continue even when the heaviest rainfall has passed.

The storm has highlighted the vulnerability of normally arid parts of the Southwest to repeated intense rainfall. Although conditions began to improve in some areas, evacuations, rescue work and infrastructure inspections continued as communities assessed the damage.
