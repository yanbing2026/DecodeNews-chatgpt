# 《South of Midnight》创意总监称，若由自己掌管预算，可能不会批准这款游戏

《South of Midnight》创意总监 David Sears 在接受 MinnMax 主持人 Ben Hanson 采访时表示，回头看，他可能不会以自己的资金决策方式批准这款游戏获得同等规模的投资。Sears 同时强调，他本人希望制作这款作品，也感谢 Microsoft 曾提供支持。他的发言将焦点引向 Xbox 过去的游戏投资模式，以及大型发行商在高成本项目上的风险判断。

## 关键回答来自一段假设性提问

Kotaku 11月2日报道，Hanson 在采访中总结 Sears 的意思：他是否感谢 Microsoft 大力支持《South of Midnight》，但如果自己负责决策，可能不会批准这项投资。Sears 回答“Yeah, probably”。这段回答并不是他否认项目价值，也不是说自己不愿参与开发。Sears 随后明确表示，他当然想制作这款游戏，并指出自己并非完全无私，因此如果花的是别人的钱，可能会更早停止资金投入。

## Sears认为长期资金模式难以持续

Sears 在采访中回顾开发期间的担忧。他说，自己曾提醒同事，某一天资助方可能会关闭资金水龙头，届时会对下游团队造成严重影响。他回忆其他开发者曾询问当时是否处于游戏投资的黄金时代，而他通常会提醒对方“暂时如此”。他并未声称自己准确预测了 Xbox 后续每项组织调整，而是表达了对大规模资金支持终有可能收缩的担忧。

## 工作室经历了微软业务重组

《South of Midnight》由 Compulsion Games 开发。Kotaku 报道称，该工作室此前属于 Xbox Game Studios，后来在 Microsoft 的业务重组中独立出去，并带走了自己的知识产权。Insider Gaming 11月3日报道，Sears 已离开 Compulsion Games，目前在游戏开发支持工作室 Orphan Toyz LLC 担任创意总监。现有报道没有披露 Compulsion 新项目是否已经签约，也没有说明其未来资金储备的具体规模。

## 游戏表现与投资决策并非同一问题

Sears 的回答讨论的是投资者在资金有限时如何评估项目，而不是对游戏创作过程作简单否定。Kotaku 报道称，Sears 将《South of Midnight》与 Double Fine 的《Keeper》一并作为 Microsoft 曾支持的项目例子；他认为这类作品可能带来声誉和文化价值，但未必能产生大量商业回报。相关说法是他对投资取舍的回顾，不等于公司公布了这两款游戏的具体亏损金额。

## Xbox投资策略仍有未解问题

Sears 认为，若从资金管理角度回看，过去的投资模式难以无限持续；但他也肯定 Microsoft 曾让团队完成作品，并称现在的 Microsoft 比他在上世纪90年代认识的公司更有人情味。采访没有提供《South of Midnight》的开发预算、销量、利润或 Microsoft 的内部投资回报数据。因此，不能仅凭这段采访得出游戏商业表现的量化结论，也不能将 Sears 的个人假设表述为 Xbox 管理层的正式决定。

# South of Midnight Creative Director Says He Probably Would Not Have Funded the Game Himself

David Sears, the creative director of South of Midnight, said in an interview that, in hindsight, he probably would not have approved the game’s funding if he had been making the investment decision with his own money. Sears also made clear that he wanted to create the game and appreciated Microsoft’s support. His comments have renewed discussion about Xbox’s past approach to funding expensive projects and the risks publishers take when they support games that may deliver cultural or reputational value without generating large commercial returns.

## The key remark came in response to a hypothetical

Kotaku reported on November 2 that Sears discussed the project with MinnMax host Ben Hanson. Hanson summarized the position as a question: Sears was grateful that Microsoft had strongly supported South of Midnight, but would he have funded it himself if he were making the decision? Sears answered, “Yeah, probably.”

The exchange was framed as a hypothetical about investment judgment. It was not a statement that Sears regretted making the game or did not believe in the creative work. He went on to say that he wanted to make it and that he was not completely selfless. In his words, he might have turned off someone else’s funding earlier if he had been responsible for the money.

## Sears questioned whether the funding model could last

Sears also described concerns he said he had raised during development. He recalled warning colleagues that someone might eventually decide to close the funding tap, with serious consequences for teams downstream. He said other directors had asked whether the industry was experiencing a golden era of game funding. His usual response, as he remembered it, was that the situation was temporary.

The comments describe his retrospective view of the funding environment. They do not show that he predicted the exact timing or details of Microsoft’s later organizational changes. Rather, he was expressing concern that a model built around substantial publisher support could eventually become financially unsustainable.

## Compulsion Games went through a corporate restructuring

South of Midnight was developed by Compulsion Games. Kotaku reported that the studio was previously part of Xbox Game Studios and later became independent as part of Microsoft’s restructuring, taking its intellectual property with it. Insider Gaming reported on November 3 that Sears had left Compulsion and was working as creative director at game-development support studio Orphan Toyz LLC.

The available reports do not establish whether Compulsion has signed a new project or how much financial runway the independent studio has. Those details remain undisclosed. The studio’s change in status is relevant to Sears’ remarks, but it should not be confused with a confirmed announcement that the company has closed or that the game’s intellectual property has been sold.

## Creative value and financial return are different questions

Sears discussed South of Midnight alongside Double Fine’s Keeper as examples of projects Microsoft supported despite expectations that they might not generate large amounts of money. He said such projects could increase Xbox’s prestige or become cultural touchstones. Those remarks are his description of the investment rationale, not audited financial disclosures.

The interview does not provide the development budget, sales total, profit or loss for South of Midnight. It therefore does not support a precise calculation of the game’s commercial performance. Sears’ comment is better understood as a reflection on how a publisher weighs creative ambition, brand value and financial return.

## Questions remain about Xbox’s investment strategy

Sears said that, looking back, the funding approach seemed unsustainable. He also praised Microsoft for allowing studios to become independent rather than simply shutting them down, describing the company as more humane than the one he remembered from the 1990s.

His remarks highlight a tension in the economics of game development: publishers may fund projects for reasons beyond immediate sales, but that support can become vulnerable when budgets rise or corporate priorities change. The interview does not establish Microsoft’s official rationale for every restructuring decision, nor does it provide internal financial data. Sears’ comments remain an individual account of the risks he perceived while making the game.
