# 联邦法官驳回特朗普政府索取哈佛招生资料诉讼

## 法院以程序理由驳回诉讼
美国马萨诸塞州联邦地区法官Myong J. Joun于11月2日批准Harvard University提出的驳回动议，驳回联邦政府要求学校交出招生资料的诉讼，但裁定为“不妨碍重新起诉”。案件涉及司法部和教育部对哈佛是否遵守联邦民权法律的调查。法院强调，这次裁决只处理政府是否充分满足强制取得资料前的程序要求，并没有判定哈佛是否存在招生歧视。

## 政府希望取得申请人层级资料
特朗普政府于2026年2月起诉哈佛，称学校没有充分配合联邦部门对招生流程的审查。政府要求的资料包括申请人层级的招生记录和个人申请文书，希望借此判断哈佛是否仍在招生中考虑种族因素。调查背景是最高法院2023年关于大学招生种族因素的裁决。政府认为哈佛拖延或拒绝提供资料；哈佛则认为政府的要求过于宽泛，并称学校遵守相关法律。

## 法官指出行政程序存在缺口
在备忘录意见中，Joun法官分别审视司法部和教育部的程序。法院认为，司法部没有在诉状所依据的材料中充分说明，已按相关法规完成要求的事前认定和通知程序；教育部也没有充分说明，在转向司法执行前采取了法规要求的进一步劝服步骤。法官指出，单纯设定期限和威胁起诉，并不必然满足自愿合规程序的要求。

## 裁决没有解决歧视争议
法官特别说明，法院没有认定哈佛是否歧视申请人，也没有决定政府能否在法律允许的范围内索取适当界定的申请人资料，更没有裁定哈佛最终是否必须提供这些资料。裁决不妨碍政府补足行政程序后重新提出诉讼。法院也没有就政府是否出于报复目的提出诉讼作出结论。

## 哈佛暂时无需按本案交出资料
美联社报道，哈佛在诉讼中反对政府的资料要求，并主张政府没有遵守适用程序。法院驳回诉讼意味着政府不能凭当前诉状取得所要求的司法强制令，但不等于哈佛永久免于任何合法资料要求。未来若政府完成必要步骤并重新起诉，法院仍可能审查新的诉状和行政记录。案件因此暂时以程序性结果告一段落，招生政策本身的争议仍未解决。

# Federal Judge Dismisses Trump Administration Lawsuit Seeking Harvard Admissions Data

## The court dismisses the case on procedural grounds
A federal judge in Massachusetts on November 2 granted Harvard University’s motion to dismiss a Trump administration lawsuit seeking admissions records. The dismissal was without prejudice, meaning the government may be able to bring another case after addressing the deficiencies identified by the court. The dispute arose from federal investigations into whether Harvard complied with civil-rights requirements in its admissions process. Judge Myong J. Joun emphasized that the decision addressed the government’s pre-enforcement procedures, not whether Harvard had discriminated against applicants.

## The government sought applicant-level records
The Trump administration sued Harvard in February 2026, alleging that the university had not adequately cooperated with federal reviews of its admissions practices. The government sought applicant-level admissions information, including individual application materials, to examine whether race continued to play a role in admissions decisions. The investigation followed the Supreme Court’s 2023 decision restricting the use of race-conscious admissions. The government argued that Harvard had delayed or withheld information. Harvard countered that the demands were too broad and said it had complied with applicable law.

## The judge identified gaps in the administrative process
In a memorandum of decision, Joun considered the Justice Department’s and the Education Department’s procedures separately. The court concluded that the complaint did not adequately establish that the Justice Department had completed the required pre-enforcement determination and notice steps. It also found that the Education Department had not sufficiently shown that it took the additional steps to encourage voluntary compliance required by its regulations before referring the matter for judicial enforcement. The judge wrote that a deadline and a threat to sue, standing alone, do not necessarily demonstrate compliance with the required process.

## The ruling does not decide the discrimination question
The judge expressly stated that the decision does not determine whether Harvard discriminated, whether the agencies may lawfully seek appropriately defined applicant-level data, or whether Harvard may ultimately be required to provide such information. The ruling also does not resolve Harvard’s allegation that the government acted with an improper or retaliatory purpose. The dismissal was without prejudice, leaving the administration free to pursue another action if it can satisfy the applicable procedural requirements and support its claims.

## Harvard does not have to produce the records under this case
The Associated Press reported that Harvard challenged the government’s demands and argued that the agencies had not followed the required process. The dismissal means the government did not obtain the judicial enforcement it sought through the current complaint. It does not establish a permanent exemption for Harvard from every lawful request for admissions information. A new case, supported by a different administrative record, could be considered on its own merits. For now, the case has ended on a procedural basis while the underlying dispute over admissions practices remains unresolved.
